tagline: The Holy One of Israel judges, saves, and promises a Servant-King
author: Isaiah son of Amoz, a prophet in Jerusalem (1:1). The New Testament quotes from all parts of the book under Isaiah's name (e.g., John 12:38-41). Many modern scholars assign chapters 40-66 to later writers.
date: Isaiah ministered about 740-681 BC, from the year King Uzziah died through the reigns of Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah.
audience: Judah and Jerusalem, threatened by Assyria and later facing exile in Babylon, as well as future generations in exile and beyond.
setting: Jerusalem during the rise of the Assyrian empire, the fall of the northern kingdom (722 BC), and Sennacherib's siege (701 BC), with prophecies reaching to the Babylonian exile and the return.
purpose: To call Judah to trust the Holy One of Israel instead of foreign alliances and idols, to announce judgment on sin, and to promise salvation through a coming King and a Suffering Servant who will bring a new creation.
structure: Judgment and hope for Judah and the nations (1-35), the historical interlude with Hezekiah (36-39), comfort and the Servant of the LORD (40-55), and the future glory of God's people (56-66).

## themes
- God's Holiness | God is "the Holy One of Israel," a title used about 25 times. Isaiah's vision (6:3) shapes the whole book.
- Judgment for Sin | Pride, idolatry, injustice, and empty religion bring God's judgment on Judah and the nations.
- Trust in God, Not Alliances | Isaiah urges kings to rely on God rather than Egypt or Assyria (30:15; 31:1).
- The Messiah King | A child born of a virgin, the Prince of Peace, the shoot from Jesse, will reign forever (7:14; 9:6-7; 11:1-10).
- The Suffering Servant | The Servant will bear the sins of many and be pierced for our transgressions (52:13-53:12).
- Comfort and Salvation | "Comfort, comfort my people" (40:1). God redeems, restores, and invites all to come (55:1).
- New Creation | God promises new heavens and a new earth (65:17).

## reflect
- Isaiah's vision of God's holiness led to confession and then commission (ch. 6). How does God's holiness affect you?
- Where are you tempted to rely on your own "Egypt" (money, people, plans) instead of God (30:15; 31:1)?
- Read Isaiah 53 slowly. Write a prayer of thanks for what the Servant did for you.
- Isaiah 40:31 promises renewed strength to those who wait on God. What are you waiting on Him for?
- Isaiah 55 invites the thirsty to come and buy without money. Who could you share this invitation with?

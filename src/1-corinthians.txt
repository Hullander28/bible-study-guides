tagline: Christ crucified for a divided, gifted, and struggling church
author: The apostle Paul, with Sosthenes (1:1).
date: About AD 53-55, from Ephesus during Paul's third missionary journey (16:8).
audience: The church in Corinth, a wealthy, diverse, and morally permissive Greek port city, which Paul founded (Acts 18).
setting: Reports from Chloe's household and a letter from the church raised problems: factions, immorality, lawsuits, marriage questions, food offered to idols, disorder in worship, spiritual gifts, and doubts about the resurrection.
purpose: To correct divisions and sin, answer the church's questions, and center everything on Christ crucified and risen, with love as the more excellent way.
structure: Divisions and the wisdom of the cross (1-4), moral problems (5-6), answers about marriage, idol food, and worship (7-14), the resurrection (15), and closing instructions (16).

## themes
- The Cross | God's wisdom looks foolish to the world but is the power of God.
- Unity | Christ is not divided; leaders are servants.
- Holiness | Believers are God's temple, bought with a price, called to flee sexual immorality.
- Christian Freedom and Love | Freedom is limited by love for others' consciences.
- The Body of Christ | Many members, many gifts, one Spirit.
- Love | Love is patient and kind and never ends (13).
- Resurrection | Christ's resurrection guarantees ours.

## chapters
@ The Word of the Cross
Paul thanks God for the Corinthians' gifts. He appeals against divisions: "I follow Paul," "I follow Apollos." "Is Christ divided?" The word of the cross is folly to the perishing but the power of God to the saved. God chose the foolish and weak to shame the wise and strong, so no one may boast except in the Lord.
? Why were the Corinthians divided?
? Why does the cross seem foolish to the world?
? Why does God choose the weak (1:27-29)?

@ Spirit-Taught Wisdom
Paul came not with eloquence but resolved to know nothing "except Jesus Christ and him crucified," so faith would rest on God's power. God's hidden wisdom is revealed by the Spirit. The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit, but "we have the mind of Christ."

@ God's Field and God's Temple
The Corinthians are still worldly, divided over leaders. Paul planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth. Each builder must take care how he builds on the foundation, Jesus Christ; work will be tested by fire. "You are God's temple and God's Spirit dwells in you."

@ Servants of Christ
Leaders are servants and stewards, judged by the Lord, not human courts. Paul contrasts the Corinthians' arrogance with the apostles' suffering, "fools for Christ's sake." As their father in the faith, he urges them to imitate him and sends Timothy.

@ Discipline in the Church
A man is living with his father's wife, and the church is proud. Paul calls for his removal so that his spirit may be saved. A little leaven leavens the whole lump; Christ our Passover lamb has been sacrificed. Do not associate with a so-called brother who is sexually immoral or greedy.

@ Bought with a Price
Believers are suing each other before unbelievers. Paul says it is better to be wronged. The unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom, "and such were some of you. But you were washed... sanctified... justified." Flee sexual immorality. Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit; glorify God in your body.

@ Marriage and Singleness
Paul answers questions about marriage. Husbands and wives should not deprive each other. Believers should not divorce, and if an unbelieving spouse leaves, the believer is not bound. Each should lead the life the Lord assigned. Singleness allows undivided devotion to the Lord.

@ Knowledge and Love
On food offered to idols: knowledge puffs up, but love builds up. Idols are nothing, and there is one God and one Lord, Jesus Christ. But not all know this, and eating can wound a weaker brother's conscience. "If food makes my brother stumble, I will never eat meat."

Paul has rights as an apostle, including financial support, but gives them up for the gospel. "I have become all things to all people, that by all means I might save some." He disciplines his body like an athlete for an imperishable wreath.

@ Flee Idolatry
Israel's wilderness failures are examples: idolatry, immorality, testing God, grumbling. "Let anyone who thinks that he stands take heed lest he fall." God provides a way of escape from temptation. Flee idolatry; you cannot share the Lord's table and demons' table. "Do all to the glory of God."

@ Worship and the Lord's Supper
Paul addresses head coverings, honoring God's created order in worship. At the Lord's Supper, divisions and selfishness shame the poor. Paul recounts Jesus' institution: "Do this in remembrance of me." Eating unworthily brings judgment; examine yourselves.

@ One Body, Many Members
There are varieties of gifts but the same Spirit, given for the common good. As a body has many parts, so the church is one body in Christ. Every member is needed; the weaker parts are indispensable. If one member suffers, all suffer together.

@ The Way of Love
Without love, gifts are nothing. "Love is patient and kind; love does not envy or boast." Love bears, believes, hopes, and endures all things. Love never ends. Now we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face. "So now faith, hope, and love abide, these three; but the greatest of these is love."

@ Orderly Worship
Pursue love and desire prophecy, which builds up the church more than uninterpreted tongues. Everything in worship should be done for building up. God is not a God of confusion but of peace. "All things should be done decently and in order."

@ The Resurrection
Paul summarizes the gospel: Christ died for our sins, was buried, and was raised on the third day, appearing to many. If Christ is not raised, our faith is futile. But He is the firstfruits. The dead will be raised with imperishable, spiritual bodies. "O death, where is your victory?" "Your labor is not in vain."

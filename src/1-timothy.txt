tagline: How to behave in the household of God
author: The apostle Paul (1:1).
date: About AD 62-64, after Paul's release from his first Roman imprisonment.
audience: Timothy, Paul's young co-worker and "true child in the faith," leading the church in Ephesus.
setting: False teachers in Ephesus promoted myths, speculations, and asceticism, and some used religion for financial gain. The church needed sound teaching and qualified leaders.
purpose: To instruct Timothy to confront false teaching, order the church's worship and leadership, care for widows and elders, and pursue godliness himself.
structure: False teaching and the gospel (1), worship and leadership (2-3), Timothy's personal ministry (4), care for groups in the church (5-6:2), and final charges about money and godliness (6:3-21).

## themes
- Sound Doctrine | Teaching that accords with the gospel produces love and godliness.
- God's Grace to Sinners | Christ came to save sinners, "of whom I am the foremost" (1:15).
- Prayer for All | God desires all people to be saved.
- Qualified Leaders | Elders and deacons must be above reproach in character and family.
- Godliness | Train yourself for godliness, which has value in every way.
- Care in the Church | Treat people as family; honor widows and elders.
- Money and Contentment | Godliness with contentment is great gain; the love of money is a root of evil.

## chapters
@ The Foremost of Sinners
Paul urges Timothy to stay in Ephesus and charge certain people not to teach false doctrine. The aim of the charge is love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and sincere faith. The law is good if used lawfully. Paul thanks Christ for appointing him, a former blasphemer, as an example of mercy: "Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the foremost."

@ Prayer for All People
Pray for all people, including kings and those in authority, so believers may live peaceful, godly lives. God desires all people to be saved. There is one mediator, Christ Jesus. Men should pray without anger; women should adorn themselves with good works. Paul gives instructions about women's roles in teaching, rooted in creation.

@ Qualifications for Leaders
An overseer must be above reproach: faithful in marriage, sober-minded, hospitable, able to teach, not a drunkard or lover of money, managing his household well, not a recent convert. Deacons must be dignified and tested. The church is the pillar and buttress of the truth. Great is the mystery of godliness: Christ manifested in the flesh.

@ Train for Godliness
In later times some will depart from the faith, forbidding marriage and foods, but everything God created is good when received with thanksgiving. Train yourself for godliness. Let no one despise your youth; set an example. Devote yourself to Scripture reading, exhortation, and teaching. Watch your life and doctrine closely.

@ Caring for the Church Family
Treat older men as fathers, younger men as brothers, older women as mothers, younger women as sisters. Honor widows who are truly in need; families should care for their own. Elders who rule well deserve double honor and fair treatment; do not receive charges without witnesses. Do not be hasty in appointing leaders.

@ Godliness with Contentment
False teachers think godliness is a means of gain. "But godliness with contentment is great gain." The love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. Flee these things; pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, steadfastness, gentleness. Fight the good fight. Charge the rich not to be haughty or trust in riches, but to be generous. Guard the deposit.

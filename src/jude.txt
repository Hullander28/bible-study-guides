tagline: Contend for the faith, and keep yourselves in God's love
author: Jude (Judas), "a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James" (1), and thus a half-brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3).
date: Probably about AD 65-80.
audience: Believers who are "called, beloved in God the Father and kept for Jesus Christ" (1), likely Jewish Christians.
setting: Ungodly people had crept into the church unnoticed, turning God's grace into a license for immorality and denying Jesus Christ.
purpose: To urge believers to contend for the faith once for all delivered, to expose and warn about false teachers, and to call believers to build themselves up, keep in God's love, and show mercy.
structure: Greeting and purpose (1-4), examples of judgment and description of false teachers (5-16), exhortations to believers (17-23), and a doxology (24-25).

## themes
- Contending for the Faith | The faith was "once for all delivered to the saints" (3).
- False Teachers | They pervert grace into sensuality and deny Christ.
- God's Judgment | Examples include Israel in the wilderness, rebellious angels, and Sodom and Gomorrah.
- Kept by God | Believers are kept for Jesus Christ, and God is able to keep them from stumbling.
- Perseverance | Build yourselves up, pray in the Spirit, keep in God's love.
- Mercy | Have mercy on those who doubt and rescue others.

## chapters
@ Contend for the Faith
Jude intended to write about salvation but felt compelled to urge believers to contend for the faith, because ungodly people had crept in, perverting grace into sensuality and denying Jesus Christ. He recalls God's judgment on unbelieving Israel, rebellious angels, and Sodom and Gomorrah. These false teachers are like Cain, Balaam, and Korah: hidden reefs, waterless clouds, fruitless trees, wild waves, wandering stars. But believers should remember the apostles' warnings, build themselves up in faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, keep in God's love, and have mercy on doubters. Jude closes praising God, who is able to keep them from stumbling.

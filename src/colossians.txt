tagline: Christ is all: the supremacy and sufficiency of Christ
author: The apostle Paul, with Timothy (1:1).
date: About AD 60-62, during Paul's imprisonment, sent with Ephesians and Philemon by Tychicus (4:7-9).
audience: The church in Colossae, a small town in Asia Minor, founded by Epaphras, whom Paul had not visited.
setting: A false teaching threatened the church, mixing Jewish rules, angel worship, mystical visions, and human philosophy, implying that Christ was not enough.
purpose: To show that Christ is supreme over all creation and the church, sufficient for salvation and growth, and to call believers to live their new life in Him.
structure: Christ's supremacy (1), warning against false teaching (2), the new life in Christ (3), and final instructions (4).

## themes
- The Supremacy of Christ | He is the image of the invisible God, Creator, sustainer, and head of the church.
- The Sufficiency of Christ | In Him dwells all the fullness of God, and we are filled in Him.
- Warning Against False Teaching | Philosophy, legalism, and mysticism cannot add to Christ.
- Union with Christ | Believers died, were raised, and are hidden with Christ in God.
- The New Self | Put off sin and put on compassion, kindness, humility, and love.
- Thankfulness | Gratitude appears in every chapter.

@ The Supremacy of Christ
Paul thanks God for the Colossians' faith, love, and hope and prays they would be filled with knowledge of God's will and walk worthy of the Lord. God delivered us from darkness into the kingdom of His beloved Son. Christ is the image of the invisible God, Creator and sustainer of all, head of the church, and reconciler through the blood of His cross. Paul rejoices in suffering for the mystery: "Christ in you, the hope of glory."

@ Complete in Christ
Paul wants believers to be rooted and built up in Christ, "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom." Beware of empty philosophy. In Christ the fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in Him. God canceled the record of debt, nailing it to the cross. Do not let anyone judge you on food or festivals; these are shadows. Human rules have no value against the flesh.

@ Seek the Things Above
Since you have been raised with Christ, seek things above. Put to death earthly sins and put off anger and lies. Put on the new self, where "Christ is all, and in all." Put on compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, patience, forgiveness, and above all love. Let the word of Christ dwell richly. Instructions for wives, husbands, children, fathers, and servants follow: work heartily as for the Lord.

@ Prayer and Gracious Speech
Masters must treat servants justly. Continue steadfastly in prayer with thanksgiving, praying for open doors for the gospel. Walk wisely toward outsiders, making the best use of time; let your speech be gracious, seasoned with salt. Paul sends greetings from Tychicus, Onesimus, Aristarchus, Mark, Luke, Demas, and Epaphras, who struggles in prayer for them.

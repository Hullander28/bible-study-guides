tagline: The prophet's marriage and God's relentless love for an unfaithful people
author: Hosea son of Beeri, a prophet to the northern kingdom (1:1).
date: About 755-715 BC, during the reigns of Jeroboam II of Israel and Uzziah through Hezekiah of Judah.
audience: The northern kingdom of Israel (often called Ephraim) in its last prosperous and then chaotic decades before Assyria destroyed Samaria in 722 BC.
setting: Israel enjoyed wealth under Jeroboam II but fell into Baal worship, political intrigue, and rapid turnover of kings.
purpose: To expose Israel's spiritual adultery, announce judgment, and display God's steadfast, covenant love that will heal and restore His people.
structure: Hosea's marriage to Gomer as a living parable (1-3), followed by oracles of Israel's sin and judgment interwoven with God's love (4-14).

## themes
- Spiritual Adultery | Israel's idolatry is pictured as a wife's unfaithfulness to her husband.
- Steadfast Love (Hesed) | God's covenant love persists despite Israel's betrayal.
- Knowledge of God | "My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (4:6). God desires steadfast love and knowledge of Him more than sacrifices (6:6).
- Judgment and Discipline | Israel will reap the whirlwind of its sin, going into exile.
- Redemption | Like Hosea buying back Gomer, God will redeem His people.
- Return and Healing | God calls Israel to return and promises, "I will heal their apostasy; I will love them freely" (14:4).

## christ
- 11:1 | "Out of Egypt I called my son" is fulfilled in Jesus' return from Egypt (Matthew 2:15).
- 3:1-5 | Hosea redeeming Gomer pictures Christ redeeming His bride at great cost (Ephesians 5:25).
- 6:6 | Jesus quotes this verse twice to rebuke the Pharisees (Matthew 9:13; 12:7).
- 1:10; 2:23 | "Not my people" becoming "my people" is applied to Gentiles in Christ (Romans 9:25-26; 1 Peter 2:10).
- 13:14 | Victory over death and Sheol is quoted in 1 Corinthians 15:55.

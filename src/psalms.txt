tagline: Israel's songbook: praise, lament, and prayer for every season
author: Many authors. David is credited with about 73 psalms; others are attributed to Asaph, the sons of Korah, Solomon, Moses (Psalm 90), Heman, Ethan, and many are anonymous.
date: Written over about 1,000 years, from Moses (about 1400 BC) to after the exile (about 500 BC or later), and compiled into five books over time.
audience: Israel gathered for worship and individuals praying in every circumstance. The Psalms have become the prayer book of the church as well.
setting: Many settings: David's flight from Saul, the temple in Jerusalem, the exile in Babylon, and the return. Some headings give specific occasions.
purpose: To give God's people inspired words for praise, thanksgiving, confession, lament, trust, and wisdom, centered on the LORD as King and on His anointed king.
structure: Five books, each ending with a doxology: Book 1 (1-41), Book 2 (42-72), Book 3 (73-89), Book 4 (90-106), and Book 5 (107-150). Psalms 1-2 introduce the collection, and 146-150 close it with praise.
plan: 150 psalms: one a day takes five months; reading five a day (for example, the day of the month plus 30, 60, 90, and 120) takes a month. For each psalm, read it on ESV.org, then come back here for the summary, questions, and your notes.

## themes
- God Is King | "The LORD reigns" over creation, nations, and history (93; 96-99).
- Praise and Thanksgiving | Many psalms call all creation to praise God for who He is and what He has done.
- Honest Lament | About a third of the psalms are laments, teaching us to bring pain, doubt, and anger to God.
- Trust and Refuge | God is a rock, fortress, shield, and shepherd for those who take refuge in Him.
- The Word of God | God's law is perfect, reviving the soul, and a lamp to our feet (19; 119).
- The Messiah | Royal psalms point to the anointed King who will rule the nations, suffer, and rise.
- Repentance and Forgiveness | Psalms like 32 and 51 model honest confession and joy in forgiveness.

## reflect
- Which psalm best matches your current season: praise, lament, confession, or trust? Pray it out loud.
- The psalmists bring raw emotions to God. What feeling are you hiding from Him that you could bring honestly?
- Try writing your own psalm, following the pattern of a lament: complaint, request, and trust.
- Psalm 1 says the blessed person meditates on God's law day and night. What would that look like in your routine?
- Many psalms remember God's past faithfulness. Make a list of what God has done for you, and praise Him for it.
- The Psalms end in praise. How can you make praise a daily habit, even in difficult seasons?

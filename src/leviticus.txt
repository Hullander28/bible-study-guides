tagline: How a holy God can live among a sinful people
author: Traditionally Moses. Over 35 times the book says "The LORD spoke to Moses," and the New Testament cites its laws as from Moses (e.g., Matthew 8:4).
date: Given at Mount Sinai in the year after the Exodus (about 1445 BC on the early date) and written down during the wilderness period.
audience: Israel's priests and people camped at Sinai, who needed to know how to worship and live as God's holy people with His presence in their midst.
setting: Entirely at Mount Sinai, picking up right where Exodus ends with the tabernacle filled with God's glory.
purpose: To teach Israel how sinful people may approach a holy God through sacrifice and priesthood, and how they should live holy lives in every area: worship, food, health, sexuality, justice, and time.
structure: Sacrifices (1-7), priesthood (8-10), clean and unclean (11-15), the Day of Atonement (16), and the Holiness Code (17-27), with blessings, curses, and vows at the end.

## themes
- Holiness | "You shall be holy, for I the LORD your God am holy" (19:2). Holiness means being set apart for God and reflecting His character.
- Atonement | Sin brings death, but God provides a substitute whose blood makes atonement (17:11).
- Access to God | The tabernacle, priests, and sacrifices show both the wonder and the cost of drawing near to a holy God.
- Clean and Unclean | Everyday laws taught Israel to distinguish life from death and to live with constant awareness of God's presence.
- Love of Neighbor | The command "love your neighbor as yourself" (19:18) is rooted in Leviticus, alongside care for the poor and the sojourner.
- Sacred Time | Sabbaths, feasts, and the Jubilee shape Israel's calendar around God's grace and provision.

- 16 | The Day of Atonement is fulfilled when Jesus enters the true holy place with His own blood (Hebrews 9:11-14).
- 16:8-10 | The scapegoat carrying sins away pictures Christ bearing our sins (Isaiah 53:6; John 1:29).
- 8-9 | Aaron's priesthood foreshadows Jesus, the sinless high priest (Hebrews 7:26-28).
- 13-14 | Jesus touches and cleanses lepers rather than being made unclean (Mark 1:40-44).
- 23 | The feasts find fulfillment in Christ: Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7), Firstfruits (1 Corinthians 15:20), and Pentecost (Acts 2).

@ The Burnt Offering
God speaks from the tent of meeting and gives instructions for the burnt offering, a male animal without blemish (from the herd, the flock, or birds for the poor). The worshiper lays a hand on its head and it is accepted to make atonement, and the whole animal is burned as a pleasing aroma to the LORD.

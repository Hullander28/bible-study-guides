tagline: The long decline to exile, and God's patient prophets
author: Not stated. Jewish tradition credits Jeremiah. It continues 1 Kings and draws on royal annals.
date: The events cover about 853-561 BC. The book was completed during the exile, after Jehoiachin's release in 561 BC (25:27).
audience: The exiles in Babylon, asking why God's people had lost the land, the temple, and the throne.
setting: The northern kingdom of Israel until its fall to Assyria in 722 BC, and the southern kingdom of Judah until Jerusalem's fall to Babylon in 586 BC.
purpose: To show that exile came because both kingdoms persistently rejected God's word through the prophets, while God remained patient, preserved a remnant, and kept a lamp burning for David's line.
structure: Elisha's ministry and the divided kingdoms (1-17), ending with Israel's fall, then Judah alone (18-25), from Hezekiah and Josiah's reforms to Babylon's destruction of Jerusalem.

## themes
- God's Patience | God sends prophet after prophet to warn His people before judgment falls (17:13-14).
- The Power of God's Word | Prophetic words come true precisely, both in blessing and judgment.
- Idolatry Leads to Exile | Both kingdoms fall because they worship other gods and refuse to listen.
- Faith in Crisis | Hezekiah spreads Assyria's threatening letter before the LORD and sees deliverance.
- Reform and Its Limits | Josiah's wholehearted reform cannot undo generations of sin; a deeper renewal is needed.
- Hope for David's Line | The book ends with the Davidic king released from prison, a small sign that God's promise lives.

## reflect
- Elisha asked for a double portion of God's Spirit. What do you most want God to do in and through you?
- Elisha's servant saw only the enemy army until his eyes were opened (6:17). What spiritual realities do you need eyes to see?
- Naaman nearly missed healing because of pride (5:11-12). Where is pride keeping you from simple obedience?
- Hezekiah spread his trouble before the LORD (19:14). What do you need to lay out before God?
- Josiah responded to God's word with tears and action. How do you respond when Scripture convicts you?
## chapters
@ Elijah and Ahaziah
Injured King Ahaziah of Israel sends messengers to ask Baal-zebub, god of Ekron, if he will recover. Elijah intercepts them: "Is it because there is no God in Israel?" Fire consumes two companies of soldiers sent to seize Elijah, but the humble third captain is spared. Ahaziah dies.
? Why was consulting Baal-zebub so offensive to God?
? What made the third captain different?
? Where are you tempted to seek help apart from God?

@ Elijah Taken Up; Elisha Succeeds Him
Elisha refuses to leave Elijah and asks for a double portion of his spirit. A chariot of fire appears, and Elijah goes up to heaven in a whirlwind. Elisha takes Elijah's cloak, strikes the Jordan, and it parts. He heals Jericho's water.

? What does the "double portion" mean (see Deuteronomy 21:17)?
? How does Elijah's departure compare with Jesus' ascension (Acts 1:9-11)?

@ Victory over Moab
Israel, Judah, and Edom march against Moab and run out of water. Elisha, for Jehoshaphat's sake, prophesies that God will fill the valley with water without wind or rain. Moab sees the water red in the sun, thinks it is blood, and is defeated.

@ Elisha's Miracles of Provision
Elisha multiplies a widow's oil to pay her debt. A Shunammite woman provides him a room, and God gives her a son. When the boy dies, Elisha raises him. Elisha makes poisoned stew harmless and feeds a hundred men with twenty loaves.

? What does the Shunammite woman's faith look like (4:26)?

@ Naaman Healed
Naaman, a Syrian commander, has leprosy. An Israelite servant girl tells him about Elisha. Elisha tells him to wash in the Jordan seven times, and he angrily almost refuses, but humbles himself and is healed. Naaman confesses the God of Israel. Gehazi greedily takes gifts and is struck with leprosy.

@ The Floating Axe Head; Chariots of Fire
Elisha makes a borrowed axe head float. When the Syrian army surrounds Dothan, Elisha prays that his servant's eyes be opened, and he sees horses and chariots of fire. The Syrian army is blinded, then fed and released. Later, Samaria endures a terrible famine under siege.

Manasseh, Hezekiah's son, reigns 55 years and does more evil than the Canaanites: idols in the temple, child sacrifice, sorcery, and shedding much innocent blood. God declares that He will wipe Jerusalem as one wipes a dish. Amon follows his father's evil.

@ Josiah Finds the Book of the Law
Young King Josiah repairs the temple, and the high priest finds the Book of the Law. When Josiah hears it, he tears his clothes. The prophetess Huldah says judgment will come, but not in Josiah's day, because his heart was tender and he humbled himself.

Josiah reads the law to all the people, renews the covenant, destroys idols and pagan shrines throughout the land (fulfilling a prophecy from 1 Kings 13), and celebrates a great Passover. No king turned to the LORD like him, yet God's anger against Judah remains. Josiah dies in battle.

Zedekiah rebels, and Babylon besieges Jerusalem. The city falls in 586 BC; Zedekiah's sons are killed before his eyes, and he is blinded. The temple, palace, and houses are burned, and the people are exiled. Years later, Jehoiachin is released from prison and eats at the king of Babylon's table.

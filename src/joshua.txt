tagline: God gives His people the promised land
author: Not stated. Jewish tradition credits Joshua with most of the book (24:26 says Joshua wrote in the Book of the Law), with later additions such as the account of his death.
date: The events run from about 1406 to 1380 BC on the traditional date. The book was likely written soon after, since Rahab was still alive "to this day" (6:25).
audience: Israel settling in Canaan, and later generations who needed to remember that the land was God's gift and that faithfulness to Him was essential.
setting: Canaan, beginning at the Jordan River opposite Jericho and moving through central, southern, and northern campaigns, ending at Shechem.
purpose: To show that God kept His promise to Abraham by giving Israel the land, and to call every generation to courageous faith and wholehearted covenant loyalty.
structure: Entering the land (1-5), conquering the land (6-12), dividing the land (13-21), and serving God in the land (22-24).

## themes
- God Keeps His Promises | "Not one word of all the good promises... had failed" (21:45).
- Courageous Faith | Joshua is told repeatedly to be strong and courageous because God is with him (1:6-9).
- Obedience and Holiness | Victory follows obedience (Jericho), and defeat follows hidden sin (Achan at Ai).
- The LORD Fights for Israel | The walls fall, the sun stands still, and enemies are routed by God's power.
- Grace to Outsiders | Rahab, a Canaanite prostitute, is saved by faith and joins God's people.
- Rest and Inheritance | God gives Israel rest in the land, a foretaste of the greater rest in Christ (Hebrews 4:8-10).
- Choosing to Serve God | "Choose this day whom you will serve" (24:15).
## verses
- Joshua 1:9 | Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go.
- Joshua 21:45 | Not one word of all the good promises that the LORD had made to the house of Israel had failed; all came to pass.
- Joshua 24:15 | But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.
## christ
- Name | Joshua and Jesus are the same name (Yeshua), meaning "The LORD saves." Jesus leads His people into a greater rest (Hebrews 4:8-10).
- 2:18-21 | Rahab's scarlet cord recalls the Passover blood, and Rahab herself is in Jesus' genealogy (Matthew 1:5).
- 5:13-15 | The commander of the LORD's army, before whom Joshua takes off his sandals, is understood by many as a pre-incarnate appearance of Christ.
- 20 | The cities of refuge picture Christ, our refuge (Hebrews 6:18).

## reflect
- God told Joshua three times to be strong and courageous (1:6-9). What situation requires that courage from you now?
- Achan's hidden sin affected the whole community. Is there anything hidden in your life that needs confessing?
- The stones of remembrance (ch. 4) reminded Israel what God had done. What "stones" can you set up to help your family remember?
- Caleb was still wholehearted at 85 (14:6-12). How do you want to be serving God when you are older?
- Joshua's final challenge was "Choose this day whom you will serve." What competing "gods" do you need to put away?
## chapters
@ Joshua Commissioned
After Moses' death, God commands Joshua to lead Israel across the Jordan, promising every place they tread. Three times God says, "Be strong and courageous," and tells Joshua to meditate on the Book of the Law day and night. The eastern tribes pledge their support.
? What is the source of Joshua's courage (1:5, 9)?
? What does 1:8 teach about the role of Scripture in success?
? How can you encourage a leader the way the people did (1:16-18)?

@ Rahab and the Spies

@ Crossing the Jordan
The priests carry the ark into the flooded Jordan, and as their feet touch the water, the river stops upstream. All Israel crosses on dry ground, while the priests stand firm in the middle.

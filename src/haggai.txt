tagline: Consider your ways: rebuild God's house
author: Haggai the prophet (1:1), a contemporary of Zechariah (Ezra 5:1; 6:14).
date: Four precisely dated messages from August to December 520 BC, in the second year of King Darius of Persia.
audience: Zerubbabel the governor, Joshua the high priest, and the returned exiles in Jerusalem.
setting: About 18 years after the first return from exile, the temple foundation lay unfinished. Discouraged by opposition and hardship, the people focused on their own houses.
purpose: To stir the people to rebuild the temple by putting God first, encourage them with His presence and future glory, and affirm God's promise through Zerubbabel, David's descendant.
structure: Four messages: a call to rebuild (1), the future glory of the temple (2:1-9), a lesson on defilement and blessing (2:10-19), and a promise to Zerubbabel (2:20-23).

## themes
- Priorities | The people built paneled houses while God's house lay in ruins.
- Consider Your Ways | Their frustration and lack were a call to reflect.
- God's Presence | "I am with you, declares the LORD" (1:13; 2:4).
- Courage | "Be strong... work, for I am with you" (2:4).
- Future Glory | The latter glory of the house will be greater than the former.
- Holiness and Blessing | Holiness is not contagious, but defilement is; obedience brings blessing.

## christ
- 2:6-9 | The shaking of the nations and the greater glory point to Christ coming to the second temple (Hebrews 12:26-28; John 2:19-21).
- 2:23 | Zerubbabel, chosen as God's signet ring, is an ancestor of Jesus (Matthew 1:12-13).
- 2:9 | "In this place I will give peace": Jesus, the Prince of Peace, taught in this temple.

## chapters
@ Consider Your Ways
The people say, "The time has not yet come to rebuild the house of the LORD." God asks, "Is it a time for you yourselves to dwell in your paneled houses, while this house lies in ruins?" They sow much but harvest little. "Consider your ways." Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the people obey and fear the LORD. God says, "I am with you," and stirs their spirits, and they begin work.

@ The Latter Glory
Older people grieve that the new temple seems like nothing compared to Solomon's. God says, "Be strong... work, for I am with you." He will shake the heavens and nations, and the latter glory will be greater than the former. A lesson about holiness shows that their offerings were defiled, but "from this day on I will bless you." God will make Zerubbabel like a signet ring.

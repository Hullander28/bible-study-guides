tagline: The fall of Nineveh: the LORD is slow to anger but will by no means clear the guilty
author: Nahum the Elkoshite (1:1), whose name means "comfort."
date: Between the fall of Thebes (663 BC, mentioned in 3:8) and the fall of Nineveh (612 BC).
audience: Judah, which had suffered under Assyrian domination.
setting: Assyria, once spared after Jonah's preaching, had returned to cruelty, destroyed Israel, and oppressed Judah. Its capital, Nineveh, seemed invincible.
purpose: To announce the certain destruction of Nineveh and comfort Judah with God's justice and protection.
structure: God's character as avenger and refuge (1), the vivid fall of Nineveh (2), and the reasons for Nineveh's doom (3).

## themes
- God's Justice | "The LORD will by no means clear the guilty" (1:3).
- God's Goodness | "The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble" (1:7).
- God's Patience Has Limits | Nineveh's earlier repentance under Jonah did not last.
- Comfort for the Oppressed | The fall of the oppressor is good news for God's people.
- God Opposes the Proud and Violent | "Behold, I am against you" (2:13; 3:5).
## verses
- Nahum 1:3 | The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty.
- Nahum 1:7 | The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble; he knows those who take refuge in him.
- Nahum 1:15 | Behold, upon the mountains, the feet of him who brings good news, who publishes peace!
## christ
- 1:15 | The herald bringing good news of peace points to the gospel of Christ (Isaiah 52:7; Romans 10:15).
- 1:7 | Christ is the stronghold for those who take refuge in Him from the coming wrath (1 Thessalonians 1:10).
- 1:2-3 | God's wrath against sin is satisfied at the cross for those who believe (Romans 3:25-26).

## chapters
@ The LORD Is Avenging and Good
The LORD is a jealous and avenging God, slow to anger but great in power, who will not clear the guilty. Mountains quake before Him. Yet "the LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble." He will make an end of Nineveh and break Judah's yoke. A messenger brings good news of peace.
? How can God be both avenging (1:2) and good (1:7)?
? What does it mean that God "knows those who take refuge in him"?
? How does Paul use the image of 1:15 (Romans 10:15)?

@ Nineveh Falls
A vivid scene: the attacker advances, chariots race through the streets, the river gates open, and the palace melts. Nineveh, once a lion's den full of prey, is plundered. "Behold, I am against you, declares the LORD of hosts."
? What details make this description so vivid?
? What does the lion's den image say about Assyria (2:11-12)?
? How should "I am against you" (2:13) warn every proud power?

@ Woe to the Bloody City
"Woe to the bloody city, all full of lies and plunder." Nineveh's prostitution and sorceries brought judgment. Like Thebes, it will fall. Its defenses are like ripe figs, its merchants like locusts. "All who hear the news about you clap their hands over you. For upon whom has not come your unceasing evil?"
? What sins does God name in 3:1-4?
? Why mention Thebes (3:8-10)?
? What does the final verse say about Nineveh's cruelty?

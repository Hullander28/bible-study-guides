tagline: Living hope for exiles who suffer for doing good
author: The apostle Peter (1:1), with the help of Silvanus (5:12).
date: About AD 62-64, from Rome ("Babylon," 5:13), shortly before Nero's persecution.
audience: Believers scattered as "elect exiles" throughout the Roman provinces of Asia Minor, largely Gentile converts.
setting: These Christians faced slander, social rejection, and suffering for their faith in a hostile pagan culture.
purpose: To encourage suffering believers with their living hope and identity in Christ, and to call them to holy, submissive, and good lives that commend the gospel, following Christ's example in suffering.
structure: Salvation and identity (1:1-2:10), living as exiles in society (2:11-4:11), and suffering faithfully (4:12-5:14).

## themes
- Living Hope | God caused us to be born again to a living hope through Christ's resurrection.
- Suffering | Trials test and refine faith; suffering for doing good follows Christ's example.
- Holiness | "You shall be holy, for I am holy" (1:16).
- Identity | Believers are a chosen race, royal priesthood, holy nation, God's own people.
- Exiles and Sojourners | Believers live as strangers in the world, with honorable conduct.
- Submission | To authorities, employers, and spouses, out of reverence for Christ.
- Humility | God opposes the proud; cast your anxieties on Him.

## chapters
@ A Living Hope
Peter blesses God, who caused us to be born again to a living hope and an imperishable inheritance. Trials test faith like fire refines gold. Prophets and angels longed to understand this salvation. So set your hope fully on grace, be holy as God is holy, and remember you were ransomed by Christ's precious blood. Love one another earnestly, born again through God's enduring word.

@ Living Stones and Christ's Example
Long for pure spiritual milk. Come to Christ, the living stone, and be built into a spiritual house. You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, to proclaim His excellencies. As sojourners, keep your conduct honorable. Submit to authorities and to masters, even unjust ones. Christ suffered as our example; He bore our sins so we might die to sin and live to righteousness.

@ Ready to Give an Answer
Wives can win unbelieving husbands by respectful conduct and inner beauty; husbands should live with their wives in an understanding way. All should have unity, sympathy, love, and humility, not repaying evil for evil. If you suffer for righteousness, you are blessed. In your hearts honor Christ as holy, always prepared to give a reason for your hope with gentleness and respect. Christ suffered once to bring us to God.

@ The Fiery Trial
Since Christ suffered in the flesh, arm yourselves with the same mind and live for God's will, not human passions. The end is near, so be sober-minded for prayer, love earnestly, show hospitality, and use your gifts as stewards of grace. Do not be surprised at the fiery trial; rejoice in sharing Christ's sufferings. Entrust your soul to a faithful Creator while doing good.

@ Humble Yourselves
Peter exhorts elders to shepherd God's flock willingly, eagerly, and by example; the chief Shepherd will reward them. All should clothe themselves with humility. "Humble yourselves... casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you." Be watchful: the devil prowls like a roaring lion; resist him, firm in faith. After a little while, God will restore and establish you.

tagline: Power in weakness: the ministry of a new covenant
author: The apostle Paul, with Timothy (1:1).
date: About AD 55-56, from Macedonia, after Titus brought news from Corinth (7:5-7).
audience: The church in Corinth and believers throughout Achaia.
setting: After a painful visit and a severe letter, many Corinthians repented, but "super-apostles" were undermining Paul's authority by boasting in eloquence, credentials, and visions.
purpose: To express joy at the church's repentance, explain the nature of true gospel ministry, urge generosity in the collection for Jerusalem, and defend Paul's apostleship by boasting in weakness.
structure: Paul's ministry and reconciliation (1-7), the collection for Jerusalem (8-9), and Paul's defense against false apostles (10-13).

## themes
- Comfort in Suffering | The God of all comfort comforts us so we can comfort others.
- The New Covenant | A ministry of the Spirit and righteousness, far more glorious than the old.
- Treasure in Jars of Clay | God's power is shown through human weakness.
- Reconciliation | God reconciled us through Christ and gives us the ministry of reconciliation.
- Generosity | Christ became poor so we might become rich; God loves a cheerful giver.
- True vs. False Ministry | Paul's integrity, suffering, and love versus self-promoting false apostles.
- Power in Weakness | "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness" (12:9).

@ The God of All Comfort
Paul blesses "the God of all comfort," who comforts us in affliction so we can comfort others. He shares that in Asia he despaired of life, learning to rely on God who raises the dead. He explains his changed travel plans: all God's promises are "Yes" in Christ.

@ Ministers of a New Covenant
The Corinthians are Paul's letter of recommendation, written by the Spirit on hearts. The new covenant is of the Spirit, not the letter; the old came with fading glory, the new with surpassing glory. Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom, and we are transformed from one degree of glory to another.

@ Treasure in Jars of Clay
Paul does not lose heart or tamper with God's word. The god of this world blinds unbelievers, but God has shone in our hearts. "We have this treasure in jars of clay." Afflicted but not crushed; struck down but not destroyed. Our light momentary affliction prepares an eternal weight of glory.

Our earthly tent will be replaced with an eternal building from God. We walk by faith, not sight, and all must appear before Christ's judgment seat. Christ's love compels us. "If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation." God reconciled us and made us ambassadors: "Be reconciled to God."

The Macedonian churches, in extreme poverty, gave generously, first giving themselves to the Lord. Paul urges the Corinthians to complete their gift. "For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though he was rich, yet for your sake he became poor." Titus and others will handle the gift with integrity.

God supplies abundance for good works, and generosity overflows in thanksgiving. "Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!"

@ Spiritual Weapons
Paul answers critics who say he is bold in letters but weak in person. "The weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds." We take every thought captive to obey Christ. Paul refuses to compare himself with others. "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord."

@ False Apostles and Paul's Sufferings
Paul fears they are being led astray by false apostles who disguise themselves as servants of righteousness, as Satan disguises himself as an angel of light. Forced to boast, Paul lists his sufferings: beatings, shipwrecks, dangers, sleeplessness, hunger, and daily anxiety for the churches.

@ The Thorn in the Flesh
Paul describes a man caught up to the third heaven. To keep him from conceit, he was given a thorn in the flesh. He pleaded three times for its removal, but the Lord said, "My grace is sufficient for you, for my power is made perfect in weakness." Paul boasts in weakness: "When I am weak, then I am strong."

@ Examine Yourselves
Paul warns he will not spare unrepentant sinners on his third visit. Christ was crucified in weakness but lives by God's power. "Examine yourselves, to see whether you are in the faith." He closes: aim for restoration, agree, live in peace, and the grace, love, and fellowship of the Trinity be with them.

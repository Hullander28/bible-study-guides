tagline: David's reign: covenant blessing, grievous sin, and lasting grace
author: Not stated. Likely compiled from records by Nathan, Gad, and others (1 Chronicles 29:29). Originally one book with 1 Samuel.
date: The events cover David's reign, about 1010-970 BC. It was compiled after the kingdom divided (around 930 BC) or later.
audience: Israel under the kings, and later the exiles, who needed to remember God's covenant with David and its promise of an eternal throne.
setting: Hebron and then Jerusalem, which David captures and makes his capital, along with the surrounding nations he subdues.
purpose: To show how God established David's kingdom, made an everlasting covenant with his house, and remained faithful even when David sinned terribly and suffered the consequences.
structure: David's triumphs (1-10), David's sin with Bathsheba (11-12), David's troubles (13-20), and an appendix of reflections on his reign (21-24).

## themes
- The Davidic Covenant | God promises David an everlasting house, kingdom, and throne (7:12-16), the foundation of messianic hope.
- Sin and Consequences | David's adultery and murder are forgiven, but "the sword shall never depart from your house" (12:10).
- Repentance and Grace | David's honest confession ("I have sinned against the LORD") brings forgiveness (12:13; Psalm 51).
- Kindness (Hesed) | David shows covenant kindness to Mephibosheth for Jonathan's sake (ch. 9).
- God's Sovereignty in Politics | Through intrigue and rebellion, God establishes and preserves David's throne.
- Worship | David dances before the ark and insists on offerings that cost him something (6; 24).

## christ
- 7:12-16 | The promise of an eternal Son of David is fulfilled in Jesus (Luke 1:32-33; Acts 2:29-36).
- 7:14 | "I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son" is applied to Christ (Hebrews 1:5).
- 5:6-9 | Jerusalem becomes the city of David, where the Son of David will die and rise.
- 9 | David's kindness to lame Mephibosheth, seated at the king's table, pictures grace to sinners in Christ.
- 15:23, 30 | David weeping as he crosses the Kidron and climbs the Mount of Olives foreshadows Jesus' path on the night of His betrayal (John 18:1).

## reflect
- David's fall began when he stayed home and kept looking (11:1-2). What situations make you most vulnerable to temptation?
- Nathan told David the truth with wisdom. Who in your life has permission to say, "You are the man"?
- David invited Mephibosheth to his table for Jonathan's sake. Whom could you show kindness to for Christ's sake?
- How does God's covenant promise to David strengthen your confidence in His promises to you in Christ?
- David refused to give God offerings that cost him nothing. What does costly devotion look like for you?

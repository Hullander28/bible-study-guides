tagline: The Lamb who was slain reigns, and He is making all things new
author: John (1:1, 4, 9), traditionally identified as the apostle John, exiled on the island of Patmos.
date: Most likely about AD 95, near the end of Emperor Domitian's reign; some scholars prefer the late 60s.
audience: Seven churches in the Roman province of Asia (1:4, 11), and through them the whole church.
setting: Christians faced pressure to take part in emperor worship and pagan trade guilds, and some were persecuted or tempted toward compromise.
purpose: To reveal Jesus Christ, encourage faithful endurance, warn against compromise, and show that God reigns, that evil will be judged, and that Christ will return to make all things new.
structure: Vision of Christ (1), letters to seven churches (2-3), the heavenly throne room and the Lamb (4-5), cycles of judgment in seals, trumpets, and bowls with interludes (6-16), the fall of Babylon (17-19:10), Christ's return, final victory and judgment (19:11-20:15), and the new creation (21-22).

## themes
- The Sovereignty of God | God sits on the throne over all history.
- The Lamb Who Was Slain | Christ conquers through His sacrificial death.
- Worship | Heaven is filled with worship of God and the Lamb.
- Faithful Endurance | Believers overcome by the blood of the Lamb and their testimony.
- Judgment | God judges evil justly in seals, trumpets, and bowls.
- Spiritual Conflict | The dragon and the beasts wage war against God's people.
- Babylon vs. the New Jerusalem | The seductive, corrupt world system versus God's holy city.
- New Creation | God will dwell with His people and wipe away every tear.

## chapters
@ The Son of Man
This is the revelation of Jesus Christ, with a blessing for those who read and keep it. John greets the seven churches from the eternal God and from Jesus, the faithful witness, who loves us and freed us by His blood. On Patmos, on the Lord's day, John sees one like a son of man among seven lampstands, with eyes like fire and a face like the sun. John falls as though dead, but Jesus says, "Fear not, I am the first and the last... I have the keys of Death and Hades."
? What blessing is promised in 1:3?
? What does each detail of the vision reveal about Jesus (1:13-16)?
? How do Jesus' words in 1:17-18 speak to your fears?

@ Letters to Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum, and Thyatira
Jesus praises Ephesus for doctrine and endurance but rebukes it: "You have abandoned the love you had at first." Smyrna, poor and persecuted, is told, "Be faithful unto death, and I will give you the crown of life." Pergamum holds fast but tolerates false teaching. Thyatira is loving and serving but tolerates "Jezebel." To each who conquers, Christ gives a promise.

? What encouragement does Smyrna receive (2:10)?
? What promises are given to those who conquer?

@ Letters to Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea
Sardis has a reputation for being alive but is dead: "Wake up." Philadelphia has little power but has kept His word; Jesus sets before it an open door. Laodicea is lukewarm, saying "I am rich" but blind to its poverty. Jesus counsels them to buy true riches and says, "Behold, I stand at the door and knock."

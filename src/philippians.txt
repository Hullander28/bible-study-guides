tagline: Joy in Christ in every circumstance
author: The apostle Paul, with Timothy (1:1).
date: About AD 60-62, during Paul's imprisonment, likely in Rome.
audience: The church in Philippi, the first church Paul planted in Europe (Acts 16), which had supported him financially.
setting: Paul writes from prison, uncertain whether he will live or die, thanking the Philippians for their gift sent through Epaphroditus.
purpose: To thank the Philippians, encourage joy and steadfastness in suffering, urge humility and unity by Christ's example, warn against false teachers, and teach contentment in Christ.
structure: Paul's circumstances and joy (1), Christ's humility as our example (2), knowing Christ above all (3), and peace and contentment (4).

## themes
- Joy | Paul uses "joy" and "rejoice" repeatedly while in chains.
- Partnership in the Gospel | The Philippians shared in Paul's ministry from the first day.
- Christ's Humility | Christ emptied Himself, took the form of a servant, and died on a cross.
- Unity | Stand firm in one spirit, having the same mind.
- Knowing Christ | Everything is loss compared to knowing Christ.
- Peace and Contentment | Prayer brings God's peace, and Christ gives strength for every situation.

## chapters
@ To Live Is Christ
Paul thanks God for the Philippians' partnership and is confident that God will complete His good work in them. He prays that their love will abound with knowledge. His imprisonment has advanced the gospel. "To live is Christ, and to die is gain." He urges them to live worthy of the gospel and stand firm.

? How did Paul's chains advance the gospel (1:12-14)?
? What does it mean to live a life worthy of the gospel (1:27)?

@ The Mind of Christ
Paul urges unity and humility: "count others more significant than yourselves." Have the mind of Christ, who emptied Himself, became a servant, and was obedient to death on a cross; God exalted Him. Work out your salvation, for God works in you. Shine as lights. Paul commends Timothy and Epaphroditus.

@ Knowing Christ
Paul warns against those who put confidence in the flesh. He had every credential but counts it all loss "because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus my Lord." He wants a righteousness from God through faith. He presses on toward the goal. "Our citizenship is in heaven."
? What did Paul once rely on (3:4-6)?

@ Rejoice and Be Content
Paul urges two women to agree in the Lord. "Rejoice in the Lord always." Do not be anxious but pray with thanksgiving, and God's peace will guard your hearts. Think on what is true, honorable, and lovely. Paul has learned contentment: "I can do all things through him who strengthens me." God will supply every need.

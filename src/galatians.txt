tagline: Freedom in Christ: justified by faith, led by the Spirit
author: The apostle Paul (1:1).
date: About AD 48-49 (before the Jerusalem Council) if to southern Galatia, or the mid-50s if to northern Galatia.
audience: The churches of Galatia, likely the cities Paul founded on his first journey (Acts 13-14).
setting: Teachers ("Judaizers") insisted Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the law of Moses to be fully accepted by God.
purpose: To defend the one true gospel of justification by faith in Christ alone, Paul's apostolic authority, and the freedom of believers who walk by the Spirit.
structure: Personal history defending Paul's gospel (1-2), doctrinal arguments for justification by faith (3-4), and practical life in freedom and the Spirit (5-6).

## chapters
@ No Other Gospel
Paul, an apostle sent by Jesus Christ, marvels that the Galatians are deserting the gospel for a different one. Even if an angel preached another gospel, let him be accursed. Paul received his gospel by revelation from Jesus, not from men. He recounts his past persecuting the church and his calling by grace.

? Why does Paul stress that his gospel came from Christ?

@ Justified by Faith, Not Works
Fourteen years later, the Jerusalem apostles affirmed Paul's gospel and did not compel Titus to be circumcised. At Antioch, Paul opposed Peter to his face for withdrawing from eating with Gentiles. "A person is not justified by works of the law but through faith in Jesus Christ... I have been crucified with Christ."

@ Abraham and the Promise
"O foolish Galatians!" Did you receive the Spirit by works or by faith? Abraham believed, and those of faith are his sons. The law brings a curse, but "Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us." The law was a guardian until Christ came. In Christ all are sons of God through faith and one in Him.
? How did the Galatians first receive the Spirit (3:2-5)?
? How did Christ become a curse for us (3:13)?
? What does 3:28 teach about unity in Christ?

@ Sons and Heirs
Before Christ we were like children under guardians. In the fullness of time God sent His Son to redeem us and adopt us, and sent His Spirit crying "Abba! Father!" Paul pleads with the Galatians not to return to slavery. He contrasts Hagar and Sarah: we are children of promise, of the free woman.
? What does it mean to be adopted as God's son (4:4-7)?
? Why would anyone return to slavery (4:9)?
? What does the Hagar and Sarah allegory teach?

@ Freedom and the Fruit of the Spirit
"For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm therefore." Accepting circumcision to be justified means falling away from grace. Faith works through love. Do not use freedom for the flesh but serve one another. Walk by the Spirit: the works of the flesh are evident, but the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

@ Bear One Another's Burdens
Restore anyone caught in sin gently. "Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." Whatever one sows, he will reap. "Let us not grow weary of doing good." Paul writes with large letters: "Far be it from me to boast except in the cross." What counts is a new creation.

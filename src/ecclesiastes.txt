tagline: Life under the sun is vapor; fear God and keep His commandments
author: "The Preacher" (Hebrew Qoheleth), "son of David, king in Jerusalem" (1:1). Traditionally Solomon, though some think a later writer used Solomon's persona.
date: If by Solomon, around 935 BC near the end of his life. Others date it later, after the exile.
audience: Anyone searching for meaning in a world marked by death, injustice, and frustration.
setting: The Preacher reflects on life "under the sun," in a fallen world, from the perspective of a wealthy and wise king who has tried everything.
purpose: To show that everything under the sun is "vanity" (vapor, fleeting and elusive) and cannot satisfy, so that we fear God, enjoy His gifts with gratitude, and live in light of His coming judgment.
structure: The Preacher explores wisdom, pleasure, work, time, injustice, wealth, and death, interweaving honest despair with calls to enjoy God's gifts, and ends with a call to remember the Creator and fear God (12).

## themes
- Vanity (Hebel) | Life under the sun is vapor: fleeting, elusive, and unable to give lasting meaning.
- The Limits of Wisdom, Pleasure, and Work | The Preacher tests them all and finds none satisfies apart from God.
- God's Sovereignty and Timing | "He has made everything beautiful in its time" (3:11).
- Death | Death levels the wise and the fool, the rich and the poor.
- Enjoyment as God's Gift | Eating, drinking, and finding joy in work are gifts from God's hand.
- The Fear of God | "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man" (12:13).

## reflect
- What have you chased "under the sun" hoping it would satisfy? What did you find?
- The Preacher says there is a time for everything. What season are you in right now, and how can you embrace it?
- How can you enjoy God's everyday gifts (food, work, relationships) with gratitude instead of grasping?
- "Remember your Creator in the days of your youth." What would it look like to remember God at your stage of life?
- How does the coming judgment (12:14) give meaning to ordinary choices?
## chapters
@ Everything Is Vanity
The Preacher declares, "Vanity of vanities! All is vanity." Generations come and go, the sun rises and sets, the wind circles, and rivers run to the sea, yet nothing is new under the sun. He set his heart to seek wisdom and found it like striving after wind.
? What does "vanity" (vapor, breath) mean?
? What cycles in nature does the Preacher describe, and why do they frustrate him?
? Why does increased knowledge sometimes increase sorrow (1:18)?
> Romans 8:20 | Psalm 39:5 | James 4:14
@ Pleasure and Work Cannot Satisfy
The Preacher tests pleasure, laughter, wine, great projects, houses, gardens, servants, wealth, and music, denying himself nothing. Yet all is vanity. Wisdom is better than folly, but both die. He hates his toil because he must leave it to another. Still, eating, drinking, and enjoying work are from God's hand.
? What did the Preacher try in search of satisfaction?
? Why did he hate his work (2:18-21)?
? How does 2:24-25 point to God as the source of enjoyment?

@ A Time for Everything
"For everything there is a season": a time to be born and to die, to weep and to laugh, to mourn and to dance. God has made everything beautiful in its time and put eternity into our hearts. Whatever God does endures forever.

@ Oppression, Envy, and Companionship
The Preacher sees the tears of the oppressed with no one to comfort them. Much work springs from envy. "Two are better than one," for if one falls the other lifts him up, and "a threefold cord is not quickly broken."

@ Fear God; the Emptiness of Wealth
Guard your steps when going to God's house; listen more than you speak, and keep your vows. Whoever loves money will never be satisfied with money. We leave this world as naked as we came. To enjoy one's wealth and work is a gift of God.

A man may have wealth, honor, and many children, yet not enjoy them. All human toil is for the mouth, yet the appetite is never satisfied. Who knows what is good for a person during his few days?

> Psalm 16:11 | Philippians 4:11-13 | John 6:35
@ Wisdom for a Crooked World
A good name is better than precious ointment. It is better to go to the house of mourning than to a feast. Consider God's work: who can straighten what He has made crooked? In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in adversity consider that God made both. There is no righteous person who never sins.

> Romans 3:23 | Job 2:10 | Psalm 90:12
@ Obey the King; Life's Mysteries
The Preacher counsels obeying the king. Because judgment against evil is not carried out quickly, people's hearts are set to do evil, yet it will be well with those who fear God. No one can find out all of God's work under the sun.

@ Enjoy Life with Those You Love
The same event, death, happens to all. While alive, there is hope: "a living dog is better than a dead lion." Eat your bread with joy, enjoy life with your spouse, and do your work with all your might. Wisdom is better than might, though it is often ignored.

@ Folly's Effects
Dead flies make perfume stink, so a little folly outweighs wisdom. The Preacher observes fools in high places, the dangers of careless work, and the value of sharpening the axe. Do not curse the king even in your thoughts.

@ Cast Your Bread on the Waters
"Cast your bread upon the waters," be generous, and diversify, for you do not know what disaster may come. Do not wait for perfect conditions. Rejoice in your youth, but know that God will bring you into judgment.

> 2 Corinthians 9:6-7 | Galatians 6:9 | 2 Corinthians 5:10
@ Remember Your Creator; the Conclusion
"Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth, before the evil days come." A poetic picture of old age and death follows: the silver cord is snapped, the dust returns to the earth, and the spirit returns to God. The conclusion: "Fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man." God will bring every deed into judgment.

tagline: Receive him as you would receive me: the gospel reconciles
author: The apostle Paul, with Timothy (1).
date: About AD 60-62, during Paul's Roman imprisonment, sent with Colossians.
audience: Philemon, a wealthy believer in Colossae who hosted a house church, along with Apphia, Archippus, and the church.
setting: Onesimus, Philemon's slave, had run away, perhaps after wronging him, and met Paul in prison, where he became a Christian. Paul sends him back with this letter.
purpose: To appeal to Philemon to welcome Onesimus back not merely as a slave but as a beloved brother, showing how the gospel transforms relationships.
structure: Greeting and thanksgiving (1-7), Paul's appeal for Onesimus (8-22), and closing greetings (23-25).
## outline
* Greeting and Thanksgiving | 1-7 | 1 | 1
- Thanks for Philemon's love and faith | 1-7

- Receive Onesimus as a brother | 8-22
- Greetings and blessing | 23-25
## themes
- Reconciliation | The gospel restores broken relationships.
- Brotherhood in Christ | Onesimus is now "a beloved brother."
- Love Over Command | Paul appeals on the basis of love rather than authority.
- Substitution | "Charge that to my account" pictures Christ's payment for us.
- Changed Lives | Onesimus ("useful") was once useless but is now useful.
- God's Providence | Perhaps he was parted for a while so Philemon might have him back forever.

## chapters
@ A Beloved Brother
Paul, a prisoner, thanks God for Philemon's love and faith that have refreshed the hearts of the saints. Rather than command, he appeals in love for Onesimus, his "child" born in prison. Once useless, Onesimus is now useful. Paul sends him back, hoping Philemon will receive him "no longer as a bondservant but... as a beloved brother." "If he has wronged you... charge that to my account." Paul is confident Philemon will do even more, and asks him to prepare a guest room.

tagline: God rescues His people, gives His law, and comes to dwell among them
author: Traditionally Moses, who is the central human figure of the book. Exodus itself records that Moses wrote down the LORD's words (17:14; 24:4; 34:27), and Jesus refers to "the book of Moses" (Mark 12:26).
date: Written during the wilderness period, roughly 1446-1406 BC on the traditional early date of the Exodus (or the 13th century BC on a later date). It covers about 80 years, from Moses' birth to the building of the tabernacle at Sinai.
audience: The generation of Israel freed from Egypt and their children, who needed to understand their redemption, their covenant with God, and how to worship Him.
setting: Egypt (Goshen and Pharaoh's court), the wilderness, and Mount Sinai, where Israel camps from chapter 19 to the end of the book.
purpose: To show the LORD as the faithful, powerful God who remembers His covenant with Abraham, redeems His people from slavery, binds them to Himself by covenant at Sinai, and comes to live among them in the tabernacle.
structure: Three movements: deliverance from Egypt (1-18), covenant and law at Sinai (19-24), and the tabernacle, with the golden-calf crisis in the middle (25-40).

## themes
- Redemption | God hears His people's cry and redeems them from slavery "with an outstretched arm" (6:6). The Exodus becomes the Old Testament's great picture of salvation.
- The Name and Character of God | God reveals Himself as "I AM" (3:14) and proclaims His name as merciful, gracious, and just (34:6-7).
- Covenant | At Sinai, the redeemed people are bound to God as "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (19:6).
- Law as a Gift | The law comes after rescue (20:2). It shows Israel how a redeemed people should live with God and with each other.
- God's Presence | The book moves from God appearing in a bush to God filling the tabernacle with His glory in the midst of His people (40:34).
- Worship and Idolatry | True worship follows God's pattern, while the golden calf shows how quickly people make gods in their own image.
- Mediation | Moses stands between God and the people, interceding for them and pointing ahead to a greater Mediator.

@ Israel Oppressed in Egypt

When harsh labor fails to stop their growth, he orders the Hebrew midwives to kill the baby boys, but the midwives fear God and refuse. Pharaoh then commands that every Hebrew son be thrown into the Nile.

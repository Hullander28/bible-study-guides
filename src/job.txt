tagline: Suffering, God's wisdom, and faith that holds on
author: Unknown. Suggestions include Job himself, Moses, Solomon, or an unnamed wise man. The book is anonymous.
date: The events seem to take place in the patriarchal era (Job offers sacrifices as head of his family and measures wealth in livestock). The date of writing is uncertain, anywhere from Solomon's time to the exile.
audience: God's people of every age who wrestle with suffering, justice, and the ways of God.
setting: The land of Uz, probably east of Israel near Edom, along with scenes in the heavenly court.
purpose: To explore why the righteous suffer, to reject the simple idea that suffering always means punishment, and to show that God's wisdom and sovereignty are worthy of trust even when His reasons are hidden.
structure: A prose prologue (1-2), poetic dialogues between Job and his three friends (3-31), Elihu's speeches (32-37), God's speeches from the whirlwind (38-41), and a prose epilogue (42).

## themes
- The Mystery of Suffering | Job's suffering is not punishment for sin. The reader knows what Job does not: his faith is being tested.
- Wrong Answers | Job's friends insist suffering always means sin. God says they have not spoken rightly (42:7).
- Honest Lament | Job pours out complaints and questions to God, yet never curses Him.
- God's Sovereignty and Wisdom | God answers not with explanations but by revealing His majesty as Creator.
- Faith Without Reasons | "Though he slay me, I will hope in him" (13:15). Job's faith is purified through suffering.
- The Need for a Mediator | Job longs for an arbiter, a witness, a Redeemer between him and God (9:33; 16:19; 19:25).

## reflect
- Job worshiped God after losing everything (1:20-21). How do you respond to God when you lose something precious?
- Job's friends were right to sit silently for seven days and wrong when they began to explain. How can you comfort a suffering friend well?
- Job brought his raw complaints to God instead of walking away. What honest lament do you need to bring to God?
- God answered Job with Himself, not explanations. Can you trust God without knowing the reasons?
- Job "saw" God in a new way through suffering (42:5). How has God revealed Himself to you through a hard season?
## chapters
@ Job's Losses
Job, a blameless and upright man in Uz, is greatly blessed. In the heavenly court Satan claims Job only fears God because of His blessings. God permits Satan to test him. In one day Job loses his oxen, donkeys, sheep, camels, servants, and all ten children. Job tears his robe, worships, and says, "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD."

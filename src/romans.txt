tagline: The gospel of God: righteousness by faith for Jew and Gentile
author: The apostle Paul (1:1), written through his scribe Tertius (16:22).
date: About AD 57, from Corinth near the end of Paul's third missionary journey.
audience: The church in Rome, made up of Jewish and Gentile believers, which Paul had not yet visited.
setting: Paul planned to bring an offering to Jerusalem, then visit Rome on his way to Spain (15:22-29). Tensions existed between Jewish and Gentile Christians.
purpose: To set out the gospel clearly: all have sinned, God justifies by faith in Christ, the Spirit gives new life, God is faithful to Israel, and the gospel transforms life and unites Jew and Gentile.
structure: Introduction (1:1-17), the need for righteousness (1:18-3:20), justification by faith (3:21-5:21), new life in Christ and the Spirit (6-8), God's faithfulness and Israel (9-11), the transformed life (12:1-15:13), and conclusion (15:14-16:27).

## themes
- The Gospel | The power of God for salvation to everyone who believes (1:16).
- Universal Sin | "All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (3:23).
- Justification by Faith | God declares sinners righteous through faith in Christ, apart from works.
- Union with Christ | Believers died and rose with Christ and are no longer slaves to sin.
- Life in the Spirit | The Spirit gives life, adoption, and help in weakness.
- God's Sovereignty and Faithfulness | God keeps His promises to Israel and shows mercy to all.
- Transformed Living | Grace leads to renewed minds, sincere love, and unity.

## chapters
@ The Gospel and Human Sin
Paul, a servant of Christ, greets the Roman believers and longs to visit them. "I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation." God's wrath is revealed against ungodliness. Though God is known through creation, people exchanged His glory for idols, so God gave them over to their desires.

@ God's Impartial Judgment

@ Righteousness Through Faith
Jews have the advantage of God's oracles, but all are under sin: "None is righteous, no, not one." By works of the law no one is justified. But now God's righteousness is revealed through faith in Jesus Christ. All have sinned and are justified freely by grace through the redemption in Christ, whom God put forward as a propitiation.

@ Abraham's Faith
Abraham was justified by faith, not works: "Abraham believed God, and it was counted to him as righteousness." David also speaks of the blessing of forgiveness. Abraham believed before circumcision, so he is father of all who believe. Hoping against hope, he trusted God to give life to the dead.

@ Peace with God
Justified by faith, we have peace with God and rejoice even in suffering, which produces endurance, character, and hope. "God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us." As sin and death came through Adam, grace and life abound through Jesus Christ.

@ Dead to Sin, Alive to God
Should we sin so grace may abound? By no means! In baptism we were united with Christ in death and resurrection. Consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God. Present yourselves to God. You were slaves of sin, now slaves of righteousness. "The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life."

@ The Struggle with Sin
Believers have died to the law and belong to Christ. The law is holy, but sin used it to produce death. Paul describes the struggle: "I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate." "Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me...? Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord!"

@ Life in the Spirit
"There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." The Spirit gives life and adoption: we cry, "Abba! Father!" Present sufferings are not worth comparing with future glory. The Spirit helps us pray. All things work together for good. "If God is for us, who can be against us?" Nothing can separate us from the love of God in Christ.

Paul grieves over Israel's unbelief. Not all descended from Israel belong to Israel; God's promise works by His choice, as with Isaac and Jacob. God has mercy on whom He wills. The potter has rights over the clay. Gentiles have attained righteousness by faith, while Israel stumbled over the stone.

@ The Olive Tree and God's Mercy
God has not rejected Israel; a remnant exists by grace. Israel's stumbling brought salvation to the Gentiles, who are grafted into the olive tree. Gentiles must not be arrogant. A partial hardening has come until the full number of Gentiles comes in, "and in this way all Israel will be saved." "Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God!"

tagline: Grow in grace and knowledge, and remember the Lord's promise
author: The apostle Peter (1:1), an eyewitness of the transfiguration (1:16-18).
date: About AD 64-68, shortly before Peter's martyrdom in Rome (1:14).
audience: The same believers who received 1 Peter (3:1).
setting: False teachers had arisen, promoting immorality, denying Christ's authority, and scoffing at His promised return.
purpose: To urge believers to grow in godly character, trust the reliable word of God, recognize and reject false teachers, and live holy lives while waiting for the day of the Lord.
structure: Growing in godliness and the reliability of Scripture (1), the danger of false teachers (2), and the certainty of Christ's return (3).

## chapters
@ Grow in Godliness
God's divine power has granted all things for life and godliness and given precious promises. So make every effort to add to faith virtue, knowledge, self-control, steadfastness, godliness, brotherly affection, and love. Peter, soon to die, wants to remind them. He was an eyewitness of Christ's majesty, and the prophetic word is more fully confirmed: no prophecy came by human will, but men spoke from God carried along by the Spirit.

@ False Teachers
False teachers will secretly bring in destructive heresies, denying the Master who bought them. God judged angels, the ancient world, and Sodom and Gomorrah, but rescued Noah and Lot, so He knows how to rescue the godly. False teachers are arrogant, sensual, greedy, promising freedom while slaves of corruption. It would have been better never to have known the way of righteousness than to turn back.

@ The Day of the Lord
Scoffers will ask, "Where is the promise of his coming?" But God's word created and judged the world. With the Lord one day is as a thousand years. He is patient, not wishing any to perish. The day of the Lord will come like a thief. "What sort of people ought you to be in lives of holiness and godliness?" Wait for new heavens and a new earth. Grow in grace and knowledge of Christ.

tagline: The weeping prophet: judgment, tears, and a new covenant
author: Jeremiah son of Hilkiah, a priest from Anathoth, who dictated much of his message to his scribe Baruch (36:4, 32).
date: Jeremiah prophesied from about 627 BC (Josiah's 13th year) until after Jerusalem's fall in 586 BC, about 40 years.
audience: Judah and Jerusalem in their final decades, the exiles in Babylon, and the nations.
setting: Jerusalem under Kings Josiah, Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah, as Babylon rose to power, through the siege and destruction of the city, and afterward in Egypt.
purpose: To call Judah to repent, announce that God would judge her through Babylon for persistent idolatry and injustice, and promise restoration after seventy years and a new covenant written on the heart.
structure: Jeremiah's call (1), warnings and pleas to Judah (2-25), conflicts and the book of consolation (26-36), the fall of Jerusalem and its aftermath (37-45), oracles against the nations (46-51), and a historical appendix (52).

## themes
- Covenant Unfaithfulness | Judah has forsaken God, "the fountain of living waters," for broken cisterns (2:13).
- Judgment Through Babylon | God uses Babylon as His instrument of discipline.
- The Deceitful Heart | "The heart is deceitful above all things" (17:9). Outward religion cannot fix inward corruption.
- True and False Prophets | Jeremiah's hard message clashes with prophets who cry "Peace, peace" when there is no peace.
- The Suffering Prophet | Jeremiah is rejected, beaten, imprisoned, and weeps over his people.
- The New Covenant | God promises to write His law on hearts and forgive sin (31:31-34).
- Hope Beyond Exile | After seventy years God will bring His people home (29:10-14).

## reflect
- God knew and appointed Jeremiah before birth (1:5). How does it change your view of your life to know God formed you for a purpose?
- What "broken cisterns" are you tempted to drink from instead of the fountain of living waters (2:13)?
- Jeremiah was honest with God about his pain (20:7-18). What honest complaint do you need to bring to God?
- Jeremiah 29:7 calls exiles to seek the welfare of their city. How can you seek the good of the place where God has put you?
- How does the new covenant (31:31-34) give you confidence in God's forgiveness and transforming work?

tagline: "I have loved you," says the LORD: a call to wholehearted worship
author: Malachi, whose name means "my messenger" (1:1).
date: About 460-430 BC, likely around the time of Nehemiah, after the second temple was rebuilt.
audience: The postexilic community in Judah, including careless priests and spiritually indifferent people.
setting: The temple stood, but worship had become cold and routine. Priests offered blemished sacrifices, men divorced their wives, people withheld tithes, and many questioned God's justice.
purpose: To affirm God's covenant love, confront hollow worship and covenant unfaithfulness, and announce the coming messenger and the day of the LORD.
structure: Six disputations in which God makes a statement, the people question it ("But you say..."), and God responds, followed by a closing exhortation.

## themes
- God's Covenant Love | "I have loved you," says the LORD (1:2).
- Honoring God in Worship | God rejects blemished offerings and halfhearted service.
- Faithful Leadership | Priests should guard knowledge and teach truth.
- Faithfulness in Marriage | "I hate divorce," says the LORD (2:16), as faithlessness.
- Generosity | Withholding tithes is robbing God; God invites them to test Him.
- God Does Not Change | "For I the LORD do not change" (3:6).
- The Coming Messenger and Day | God will send His messenger and Elijah before the great day.

- 1:11 | God's name great among the nations is fulfilled through the gospel of Christ.

## chapters
@ I Have Loved You
God says, "I have loved you," but the people ask, "How have you loved us?" God points to His choosing of Jacob over Esau. Then He rebukes priests who despise His name by offering blind, lame, and sick animals. "Offer that to your governor." God's name will be great among the nations.

? What do blemished offerings reveal about the heart (1:8, 13)?
? How does 1:11 anticipate worldwide worship?

@ Unfaithful Priests and Spouses
God rebukes priests for not honoring Him; a true priest's lips guard knowledge. The people are faithless to one another, marrying foreign idol-worshipers and divorcing the wife of their youth. "For the man who does not love his wife but divorces her... covers his garment with violence." They weary God by saying evildoers are good.

@ The Messenger and the Book of Remembrance
"Behold, I send my messenger, and he will prepare the way before me." The Lord will come suddenly like a refiner's fire. "For I the LORD do not change." God calls them to return and bring the full tithe: "Put me to the test." Those who feared the LORD spoke together, and a book of remembrance was written before Him.

@ The Sun of Righteousness
The day is coming, burning like an oven for the arrogant. "But for you who fear my name, the sun of righteousness shall rise with healing in its wings." Remember the law of Moses. God will send Elijah before the great and awesome day, to turn the hearts of fathers to children and children to fathers.

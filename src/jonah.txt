tagline: A reluctant prophet and the God whose mercy reaches even enemies
author: The book is anonymous and tells the story of Jonah son of Amittai, a prophet from Gath-hepher in Galilee who served under Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25).
date: Jonah ministered around 790-750 BC. The book may have been written then or somewhat later.
audience: Israel, which needed to see God's compassion for the nations and its own hard-heartedness.
setting: Jonah is sent to Nineveh, capital of Assyria, the brutal enemy of Israel, but flees by ship toward Tarshish.
purpose: To reveal God's sovereign mercy toward all peoples, including Israel's enemies, and to expose the prejudice and self-righteousness of His own people.
structure: Jonah flees (1), prays in the fish (2), preaches and Nineveh repents (3), and is angry at God's mercy (4).

## themes
- God's Sovereignty | God commands the storm, the fish, the plant, the worm, and the wind.
- God's Mercy to the Nations | God cares for pagan sailors and a violent enemy city.
- Running from God | Jonah learns that no one can flee from the presence of the LORD.
- Repentance | Nineveh, from king to cattle, turns from evil, and God relents.
- Salvation Belongs to the LORD | Jonah confesses this in the belly of the fish (2:9).
- Self-Righteousness | Jonah is angrier about a plant than about 120,000 people.

## christ
- 1:17 | Jesus calls His death and resurrection "the sign of Jonah": three days and nights in the heart of the earth (Matthew 12:40).
- 3:5-10 | "Something greater than Jonah is here" (Matthew 12:41). Nineveh repented at Jonah's preaching; we have a greater preacher.
- 1:12-15 | Jonah thrown into the sea to calm the storm pictures one dying so that others are saved.
- 4:11 | God's compassion for the nations is fulfilled in the Great Commission (Matthew 28:19).

## chapters
@ Jonah Flees
God tells Jonah to go to Nineveh, but he flees to Tarshish. God hurls a great storm. The pagan sailors pray while Jonah sleeps. Lots fall on Jonah, who confesses that he fears the LORD who made the sea and tells them to throw him overboard. The sea calms, the sailors fear the LORD, and God appoints a great fish to swallow Jonah.
? Why might Jonah have fled from God's command?
? How do the pagan sailors act more righteously than Jonah?
? How does Jonah's sacrifice to save the sailors foreshadow Christ?

@ Jonah's Prayer
From the belly of the fish, Jonah prays a psalm of thanksgiving. He describes sinking to the depths and God bringing up his life from the pit. "Those who pay regard to vain idols forsake their hope of steadfast love... Salvation belongs to the LORD!" The fish vomits Jonah onto dry land.
? Why does Jonah thank God while still in the fish?
? What does 2:8 say about idols?
? How does 2:9 summarize the gospel?

@ Nineveh Repents
God speaks a second time, and Jonah goes. He proclaims, "Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!" The people believe God, fast, and put on sackcloth, from the greatest to the least. The king calls everyone to turn from violence. God sees and relents from the disaster.
? What does God's second call show about His grace (3:1)?
? How did Nineveh respond to a short message?
? What does Jesus say about Nineveh's repentance (Matthew 12:41)?
> Matthew 12:41 | Jeremiah 18:7-8 | Luke 11:30
@ Jonah's Anger
Jonah is furious: "I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful." He asks to die. Outside the city, God appoints a plant to shade him, then a worm to kill it, and a scorching wind. Jonah pities the plant, and God asks, "Should not I pity Nineveh, that great city," with more than 120,000 people and many animals?
? Why is Jonah angry about God's mercy (4:2)?
? What is God teaching with the plant?
? How would you answer God's final question?

tagline: Jesus is better: the great high priest of a better covenant
author: Unknown. Suggestions include Paul, Barnabas, Apollos, and others. The author knew Timothy (13:23) and wrote with polished Greek and deep knowledge of the Old Testament.
date: Probably before AD 70, since the temple sacrifices seem to be still ongoing (10:1-2).
audience: Jewish Christians, possibly in or near Rome (13:24), who were tempted under persecution to drift back to Judaism.
setting: The readers had endured earlier persecution but were growing weary, neglecting to meet, and in danger of falling away.
purpose: To show the supremacy of Jesus over angels, Moses, the priesthood, and the old covenant sacrifices, and to urge believers to hold fast, draw near, and endure by faith.
structure: Christ superior to angels and Moses (1-4), Christ's superior priesthood (5-7), the better covenant and sacrifice (8-10), the life of faith and endurance (11-12), and final exhortations (13). Five warning passages run throughout.

## themes
- The Supremacy of Christ | Jesus is greater than prophets, angels, Moses, Aaron, and the old covenant.
- Christ Our High Priest | He sympathizes with our weakness and always lives to intercede.
- The Once-for-All Sacrifice | Christ's blood accomplishes what animal sacrifices never could.
- The New Covenant | God writes His laws on hearts and remembers sins no more.
- Warnings Against Drifting | Do not neglect so great a salvation or harden your heart.
- Faith | "Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen" (11:1).
- Endurance | Run the race looking to Jesus, accepting the Father's discipline.

@ The Son Superior to Angels
Long ago God spoke through the prophets, but now He has spoken by His Son, heir of all things, through whom He made the world, the radiance of His glory and exact imprint of His nature, who upholds all things and made purification for sins. The Son is superior to angels: they worship Him, and He is enthroned forever.

@ So Great a Salvation
Pay closer attention lest we drift away; how shall we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? Jesus was made a little lower than the angels to taste death for everyone. He is not ashamed to call us brothers. He shared flesh and blood to destroy the devil and deliver those enslaved by fear of death, and to become a merciful high priest who helps those tempted.

Consider Jesus, faithful over God's house as a Son, while Moses was faithful as a servant. Psalm 95 warns, "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts," as Israel did in the wilderness. Exhort one another every day so no one is hardened by sin's deceitfulness.

We have a great high priest, Jesus, who sympathizes with our weaknesses and was tempted in every way, yet without sin. Let us draw near with confidence to the throne of grace.

@ A Priest Like Melchizedek
Every high priest is chosen to offer sacrifices and deal gently with the ignorant. Christ was appointed by God as a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek. In His earthly life He prayed with loud cries and tears and learned obedience through suffering. The readers have become dull and need milk instead of solid food.

@ Hope as an Anchor
Let us go on to maturity. The author warns that those who have tasted God's goodness and fall away cannot be restored to repentance, yet he is confident of better things for his readers. God's promise to Abraham was confirmed by an oath, giving us a hope that is a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul, where Jesus has gone as forerunner.

@ A Permanent Priesthood
Melchizedek, king of righteousness and peace, blessed Abraham and received a tithe from him. Jesus' priesthood is like his: not by descent, but by the power of an indestructible life. The law made nothing perfect, but Jesus is the guarantor of a better covenant. "He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them."

@ A Better Covenant
Jesus is a minister in the true tent set up by the Lord, of which the earthly tabernacle was a copy and shadow. He mediates a better covenant enacted on better promises. The author quotes Jeremiah 31: God will write His laws on hearts and remember sins no more. The old covenant is becoming obsolete.

@ The Blood of Christ
The tabernacle's arrangement showed that the way into the holy places was not yet open. Christ entered once for all by His own blood, securing eternal redemption. His blood purifies our conscience to serve the living God. "Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins." Christ will appear a second time to save those eagerly waiting for Him.

> Leviticus 16 | Leviticus 17:11 | 1 Peter 1:18-19
@ Draw Near and Hold Fast
The law's sacrifices could never take away sins, but by a single offering Christ has perfected forever those being sanctified. Therefore, let us draw near with a true heart, hold fast our confession, and stir one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together. A warning follows against deliberate sin. Remember your earlier endurance; do not throw away your confidence.

@ The Hall of Faith
"Faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen." Without faith it is impossible to please God. By faith Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Rahab, and many others obeyed God, some seeing deliverance and others suffering and dying, all looking forward to a better country.

@ Run with Endurance
Surrounded by so great a cloud of witnesses, lay aside every weight and sin and run with endurance, looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who endured the cross for the joy set before Him. God disciplines His children for our good. Strive for peace and holiness. We have come not to Mount Sinai but to Mount Zion. Our God is a consuming fire, and we receive an unshakable kingdom.

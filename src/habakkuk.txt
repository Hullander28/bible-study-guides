tagline: From "How long?" to "Yet I will rejoice": living by faith
author: Habakkuk the prophet (1:1).
date: Probably about 609-605 BC, shortly before the Babylonians (Chaldeans) invaded Judah.
audience: Judah, though the book is mostly a dialogue between the prophet and God.
setting: Judah under Jehoiakim was full of violence and injustice, and Babylon was rising as a world power.
purpose: To wrestle honestly with God's ways in judgment, and to show that the righteous live by faith, trusting and rejoicing in God even when circumstances collapse.
structure: Habakkuk's first complaint and God's answer (1:1-11), the second complaint (1:12-2:1), God's answer with five woes (2:2-20), and Habakkuk's prayer of faith (3).

## themes
- Honest Questions | Habakkuk brings his confusion directly to God.
- God's Sovereignty | God raises up Babylon for His purposes and will also judge it.
- Faith | "The righteous shall live by his faith" (2:4).
- Patience | "If it seems slow, wait for it; it will surely come" (2:3).
- Judgment on Pride | Five woes condemn greed, violence, exploitation, and idolatry.
- God's Glory | "The earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD" (2:14).
- Joy in Hardship | Habakkuk rejoices in God even with no figs, fruit, or flocks (3:17-18).

## chapters
@ How Long, O LORD?
Habakkuk complains: "How long shall I cry for help, and you will not hear?" Violence and injustice fill Judah. God answers that He is raising up the Chaldeans, a fierce nation, to judge Judah. Habakkuk is shocked: how can a holy God use people more wicked than Judah?
? What was Habakkuk's first complaint?
? Why was God's answer so surprising (1:5-6)?
? How does Habakkuk hold onto God's character while questioning (1:12-13)?
> Psalm 13:1-2 | Acts 13:41 | Jeremiah 5:15-17
@ The Righteous Shall Live by Faith
Habakkuk stations himself to watch. God says, "Write the vision; make it plain." It will surely come. The proud soul is not upright, "but the righteous shall live by his faith." Five woes condemn Babylon's greed, unjust gain, violence, degradation of others, and idolatry. "The LORD is in his holy temple; let all the earth keep silence before him."
? Why does God tell Habakkuk to write the vision (2:2-3)?
? How does Paul use 2:4 (Romans 1:17)?
? What contrast does 2:18-20 draw between idols and God?
> Romans 1:17 | Hebrews 10:37-38 | Psalm 46:10
@ Yet I Will Rejoice
Habakkuk prays, "In wrath remember mercy." He recounts God's mighty acts, like the Exodus, in which God marched out to save His people. He trembles, but resolves: "Though the fig tree should not blossom... yet I will rejoice in the LORD... God, the Lord, is my strength; he makes my feet like the deer's."
? Why does remembering God's past acts help Habakkuk?
? What does it mean to "remember mercy" in wrath (3:2)?
? How is joy in 3:17-19 possible when everything fails?

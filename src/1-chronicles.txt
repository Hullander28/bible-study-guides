tagline: Israel's story retold: David, worship, and God's covenant people
author: Not stated. Jewish tradition credits Ezra, and the style matches Ezra-Nehemiah. The writer is often called "the Chronicler." 1 and 2 Chronicles were originally one book.
date: Written after the return from exile, probably around 450-400 BC. The genealogies extend several generations past Zerubbabel (3:19-24).
audience: The Jews who returned from Babylon to rebuild Jerusalem, who needed to know they were still God's people, connected to Adam, Abraham, and David.
setting: The narrative focuses on David's reign in Jerusalem (about 1010-970 BC), but it was written for a small, struggling post-exilic community.
purpose: To reassure the returned exiles of their identity and God's covenant with David, and to center their life on the temple and true worship of the LORD.
structure: Genealogies from Adam to the returned exiles (1-9), and the reign of David with special focus on the ark, the covenant, and preparations for the temple (10-29).

## themes
- Identity and Continuity | The genealogies connect the returned exiles to all of God's history, from Adam onward.
- The Davidic Covenant | God's promise of an everlasting throne to David's offspring gives hope for the future (17:11-14).
- Worship at the Center | The ark, the Levites, music, and temple preparations dominate David's story.
- Seeking God | Those who seek the LORD find Him; those who forsake Him are cast off (28:9).
- All Israel | The writer emphasizes the unity of all twelve tribes as God's people.
- God's Sovereignty and Generosity | Everything belongs to God, and our giving simply returns what is His (29:11-14).

## christ
- 1-3 | The genealogy from Adam through Judah to David is continued in Matthew 1 and Luke 3, leading to Jesus.
- 17:11-14 | God's promise to establish the throne of David's son forever is fulfilled in Christ (Luke 1:32-33; Hebrews 1:5).
- 21:26 | Fire from heaven accepting David's sacrifice on the future temple mount anticipates the once-for-all sacrifice of Christ.

## reflect
- The genealogies remind us that God knows people by name. How does it feel to know you are known by God?
- Jabez asked God boldly for blessing (4:10). What do you need to ask God for?
- David learned to move the ark God's way after Uzzah's death. Are you doing God's work God's way?
- David gave generously and said, "All things come from you" (29:14). How does that perspective change your giving?
- David prepared materials for a temple he would never see. What could you prepare now for the next generation?

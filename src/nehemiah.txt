tagline: Rebuilding the walls and renewing the people
author: Nehemiah, whose first-person memoirs make up much of the book (1:1). Ezra may have compiled the final form.
date: The events take place 445-about 430 BC, under the Persian king Artaxerxes I. Written soon after.
audience: The Jewish community in Jerusalem after the exile.
setting: Susa, the Persian capital, and Jerusalem, whose walls lay in ruins about 90 years after the first return.
purpose: To show how God used Nehemiah's prayerful, courageous leadership to rebuild Jerusalem's walls despite opposition, and how the people renewed their covenant with God through His word.
structure: Rebuilding the walls (1-7) and renewing the people (8-13).

## themes
- Prayer and Action | Nehemiah prays constantly, even quick prayers before the king (2:4), and then works hard.
- Leadership | Nehemiah models vision, organization, courage, integrity, and care for the poor.
- Opposition | Mockery, threats, plots, and slander cannot stop God's work when His people persevere.
- The Word of God | The people gather to hear the law and respond with weeping, joy, and covenant commitment.
- Joy | "The joy of the LORD is your strength" (8:10).
- Ongoing Reform | Even after revival, the people drift, and Nehemiah must confront sin again.

## christ
- 2 | Nehemiah leaves a royal palace to restore a ruined city, a picture of Christ leaving glory to rebuild His people.
- 9 | The Levites' prayer recounts God's faithfulness despite Israel's failure, pointing to the need for a new covenant fulfilled in Christ.
- 13 | Nehemiah's reforms fail to change hearts permanently, showing the need for the Spirit-given new heart that Christ provides (Ezekiel 36:26).

## reflect
- Nehemiah wept, fasted, and prayed before he acted. What broken thing burdens your heart enough to pray about it?
- Nehemiah prayed a quick prayer in a crucial moment (2:4). How can you practice "arrow prayers" during your day?
- Each family repaired the wall near their own house (ch. 3). What part of God's work is near you?
- Nehemiah refused to "come down" from his work. What distractions pull you away from what God has called you to?
- The people wept and rejoiced at God's word. When did Scripture last move you that way?
## chapters
@ Nehemiah's Prayer
Nehemiah, cupbearer to the Persian king in Susa, hears that Jerusalem's walls are broken down and its gates burned. He weeps, mourns, fasts, and prays for days, confessing Israel's sins and asking God to remember His promise and grant him favor with the king.

? What does Nehemiah base his request on (1:8-9)?

@ Nehemiah Sent to Jerusalem
The king notices Nehemiah's sadness. Nehemiah prays silently, then asks to rebuild Jerusalem. The king grants his requests "for the good hand of my God was upon me." Nehemiah secretly inspects the walls by night and rallies the people: "Let us rise up and build." Sanballat and Tobiah mock.

@ The Builders of the Wall
A list records who built each section of the wall and gate: priests, goldsmiths, perfumers, rulers, daughters, and families, often near their own homes. Some nobles "would not stoop to serve."

@ Opposition and Protection
Sanballat and Tobiah mock: "If a fox goes up on it he will break down their stone wall!" The people pray and keep building, and the wall reaches half its height. When enemies plot to attack, Nehemiah posts guards and urges, "Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome." Workers build with one hand and hold a weapon with the other.
? How does Nehemiah respond to mockery and threats?
? What is the balance between prayer and practical preparation (4:9)?
? What spiritual "weapons" do you keep in hand (Ephesians 6:10-18)?

@ Injustice Among the People
The poor cry out because wealthy Jews are charging interest and enslaving their brothers' children. Nehemiah is angry, confronts the nobles, and they promise to restore what they took. Nehemiah himself refuses the governor's food allowance out of fear of God.
? Why was the internal problem as dangerous as outside enemies?
? How does Nehemiah's personal example strengthen his rebuke?
? Where might God call you to give up a right for others' sake?

@ The Wall Completed
Enemies try to lure Nehemiah away, spread false rumors, and hire a prophet to frighten him into hiding in the temple. Nehemiah answers, "I am doing a great work and I cannot come down." The wall is finished in 52 days, and the enemies realize the work was done with God's help.

? How does Nehemiah discern the false prophet (6:12)?
? How did the wall's completion affect the surrounding nations (6:16)?

@ Guards and the Genealogy
Nehemiah appoints his brother Hanani, "a more faithful and God-fearing man than many," to govern Jerusalem and sets guards at the gates. The city is large but few people live in it. He finds the genealogy of the first returnees, which is repeated.

@ The People Confess
The Israelites fast, confess their sins, and read the law. The Levites lead a long prayer recounting God's faithfulness from creation to the exile and Israel's repeated rebellion. "You are a God ready to forgive, gracious and merciful." They make a firm covenant in writing.

@ Nehemiah's Final Reforms
After returning from Persia, Nehemiah finds that Tobiah has been given a room in the temple, the Levites have not been paid, the Sabbath is being broken, and Jews have married foreign wives. He throws out Tobiah's belongings, restores the tithes, shuts the gates on the Sabbath, and confronts the mixed marriages. "Remember me, O my God, for good."

? How does Nehemiah respond to each problem?
? What does this chapter show about the need for a heart change only God can give?

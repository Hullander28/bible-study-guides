tagline: God's hidden hand preserves His people
author: Unknown. Suggestions include Mordecai or a Jew in Persia familiar with court life.
date: The events take place during the reign of Xerxes I (Ahasuerus), 486-465 BC, mostly 483-473 BC. It was written sometime after, perhaps around 460-350 BC.
audience: Jews living in the Persian Empire after the exile, and later all Jews who celebrate Purim.
setting: Susa, the Persian capital, among the Jews who did not return to Judah.
purpose: To show God's providential protection of His people even when He seems absent, and to explain the origin of the Feast of Purim.
structure: Esther becomes queen (1-2), Haman's plot (3-4), the reversal (5-7), and the Jews' deliverance and Purim (8-10).

## themes
- Providence | God is never named in Esther, yet "coincidences" like a sleepless night reveal His hidden hand.
- Courage | Esther risks her life to plead for her people: "If I perish, I perish" (4:16).
- Reversal | The gallows Haman builds for Mordecai is used for himself, and mourning turns into feasting (9:22).
- God's Faithfulness to His People | Despite exile, God protects the Jews, keeping His promises to Abraham.
- Pride and Humility | Haman's pride brings his downfall, while Mordecai is exalted.

## reflect
- God is not named in Esther, but He is everywhere in it. Where have you seen God's hidden hand in your life?
- Mordecai asked Esther, "Who knows whether you have come... for such a time as this?" What position or opportunity has God placed you in for His purposes?

## chapters
@ Queen Vashti Deposed
King Ahasuerus (Xerxes) holds a lavish 180-day display of wealth followed by a feast. When he summons Queen Vashti to show off her beauty, she refuses. On his advisers' counsel, Vashti is deposed, and a decree goes out that every man be master of his household.
? What does this chapter reveal about the Persian court?
? Why do you think Vashti refused?
? How does God set up His plan even through a king's anger?

@ Haman's Plot
Haman is promoted, and all bow to him except Mordecai, a Jew. Enraged, Haman casts lots (pur) and persuades the king to issue a decree to destroy all Jews on a single day. The king and Haman sit down to drink, but the city of Susa is thrown into confusion.

@ Haman Hanged
At the second banquet, Esther reveals her identity and pleads for her people, naming Haman as the enemy. The furious king goes out. Haman begs Esther for his life, and the king returns to find him on her couch. Haman is hanged on the gallows he built for Mordecai.

@ The King's Edict for the Jews
Esther receives Haman's house, and Mordecai is given the king's signet ring. Esther pleads again, and since a Persian law cannot be revoked, a new decree allows the Jews to defend themselves. The Jews rejoice, and many people declare themselves Jews.

tagline: Let justice roll down like waters
author: Amos, a shepherd and dresser of sycamore figs from Tekoa in Judah, called to prophesy to Israel (1:1; 7:14-15).
date: About 760 BC, "two years before the earthquake," during the reigns of Uzziah of Judah and Jeroboam II of Israel.
audience: The northern kingdom of Israel, especially its wealthy elite in Samaria and worshipers at Bethel.
setting: Israel was prosperous and secure under Jeroboam II, but its wealth was built on oppression of the poor, and its worship was empty ritual.
purpose: To announce God's judgment on Israel for social injustice and false worship, call them to seek the LORD and live, and promise future restoration of David's fallen booth.
structure: Oracles against the nations, ending with Israel (1-2), sermons against Israel (3-6), five visions of judgment (7-9:10), and a promise of restoration (9:11-15).

## themes
- Social Justice | God condemns selling the needy, trampling the poor, and corrupt courts.
- Empty Religion | God hates feasts and offerings that come from unjust lives.
- Privilege and Accountability | "You only have I known... therefore I will punish you" (3:2).
- Seek the LORD and Live | God repeatedly calls Israel to return.
- The Day of the LORD | It will be darkness, not light, for the complacent.
- God's Sovereignty Over Nations | God judges all nations by His moral standard.
- Restoration | God will raise David's fallen booth and restore His people.

## reflect
- Amos was a farmer God called to prophesy. How might God use you, even if you feel ordinary?
- Where do you see injustice that God cares about, and how might you respond?
- Is any of your worship disconnected from how you treat others?
- How does prosperity tempt you to complacency (6:1)?
- What would it look like to "seek the LORD and live" this week?

@ Judgment on the Nations
"The LORD roars from Zion." Amos announces judgment on Damascus, Gaza, Tyre, Edom, and Ammon, each "for three transgressions... and for four." Their crimes include cruelty in war, selling people as slaves, and pitiless violence.

@ Judgment on Judah and Israel
Moab is judged, then Judah for rejecting God's law. Then Amos turns to Israel: they sell the righteous for silver and the needy for sandals, trample the poor, and practice sexual immorality and idolatry. God recalls all He did for them, and no one will escape.

@ Privilege and Punishment
"You only have I known of all the families of the earth; therefore I will punish you for all your iniquities." A series of questions shows cause and effect: when the lion roars, the prophet must speak. Samaria's luxury will be destroyed.

@ Yet You Did Not Return
Amos condemns the wealthy women of Samaria, "cows of Bashan," who oppress the poor. God sent famine, drought, blight, plague, and defeat, yet five times says, "Yet you did not return to me." Therefore, "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel!"

@ Seek the LORD and Live
Amos laments for fallen Israel. "Seek me and live." People hate the one who speaks truth at the gate and trample the poor. "Woe to you who desire the day of the LORD!" It is darkness, not light. God hates their feasts: "But let justice roll down like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream."

@ The Plumb Line
Amos sees visions of locusts and fire and intercedes, and God relents. Then God shows a plumb line: Israel is crooked and will be judged. Amaziah the priest tells Amos to go home. Amos replies, "I was no prophet... but the LORD took me from following the flock."

tagline: Sound doctrine that produces good works
author: The apostle Paul (1:1).
date: About AD 62-64, between Paul's two Roman imprisonments, around the time of 1 Timothy.
audience: Titus, a Gentile co-worker whom Paul left in Crete to organize the churches.
setting: Crete's culture was known for dishonesty and laziness (1:12). False teachers, especially "of the circumcision party," were upsetting households.
purpose: To instruct Titus to appoint qualified elders, silence false teachers, and teach sound doctrine that leads every group in the church to godly living and good works, grounded in God's grace.
structure: Appointing elders and rebuking false teachers (1), teaching godly living rooted in grace (2), and doing good in society as those saved by mercy (3).

## themes
- Sound Doctrine | Teaching that is healthy and leads to godliness.
- Qualified Leaders | Elders must be above reproach and hold firm to the word.

- Good Works | Mentioned repeatedly as the fruit of true faith.
- Adorning the Gospel | Godly behavior makes the teaching about God attractive.
- Salvation by Mercy | God saved us by His mercy through washing and renewal by the Spirit.

## chapters
@ Elders and False Teachers
Paul greets Titus and reminds him why he was left in Crete: to appoint elders in every town. An elder must be above reproach, faithful, not arrogant or greedy, hospitable, self-controlled, and holding firm to the trustworthy word, able to instruct and rebuke. False teachers must be silenced; they profess to know God but deny Him by their works.

@ Grace That Trains Us
Teach what accords with sound doctrine: older men, older women, younger women, younger men, and servants each receive instruction for godly living, so that the word of God may not be reviled and the gospel may be adorned. "For the grace of God has appeared... training us to renounce ungodliness." We wait for the appearing of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who redeemed a people zealous for good works.

@ Saved by Mercy
Remind believers to submit to rulers, be ready for every good work, speak evil of no one, and show courtesy to all. We ourselves were once foolish and enslaved. "But when the goodness and loving kindness of God our Savior appeared, he saved us, not because of works... but according to his own mercy, by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit." Devote yourselves to good works; avoid foolish controversies and divisive people.

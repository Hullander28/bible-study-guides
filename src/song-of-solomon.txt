tagline: The beauty of love between husband and wife
author: Solomon is named in the title (1:1), which can mean "by," "for," or "about" Solomon. Traditionally Solomon wrote it; he wrote 1,005 songs (1 Kings 4:32).
date: Around the time of Solomon's reign, about 970-930 BC, if Solomon is the author.
audience: Israel, and all who would celebrate and guard the gift of romantic and marital love.
setting: The countryside of Israel (vineyards, gardens, Lebanon) and Jerusalem, in a series of poems between a woman (the Shulammite) and her beloved, with a chorus of "daughters of Jerusalem."
purpose: To celebrate the goodness of love, desire, and intimacy within marriage as God's good gift, and to call for patience and faithfulness in love. Jewish and Christian readers have also seen in it a picture of God's love for His people.
structure: A series of love poems: longing and courtship (1:1-3:5), the wedding (3:6-5:1), struggles and reunion in marriage (5:2-8:4), and the strength of love (8:5-14).

## themes
- The Goodness of Love and Desire | Romantic and physical love in marriage are celebrated as God's good gifts.
- Exclusive Commitment | "My beloved is mine, and I am his" (2:16). True love is faithful and exclusive.
- Patience in Love | "Do not stir up or awaken love until it pleases" (2:7; 3:5; 8:4).
- Mutual Delight and Praise | Husband and wife speak words of admiration and affection to each other.
- The Strength of Love | "Love is strong as death... many waters cannot quench love" (8:6-7).

tagline: A downward spiral, and the God who keeps rescuing
author: Not stated. Jewish tradition credits Samuel. The repeated line "in those days there was no king in Israel" suggests it was written early in the monarchy.
date: The events span roughly 1380-1050 BC, between Joshua's death and the rise of Saul. It was likely written around 1000 BC.
audience: Israel in the early monarchy, to show why the nation needed a righteous king and how dangerous it is to abandon God.
setting: Canaan during roughly three centuries of tribal life, with no central government and constant threats from surrounding peoples.
purpose: To show the destructive cycle of sin, oppression, crying out, and deliverance, to display God's patient mercy in raising up judges, and to expose the need for a faithful king.
structure: An introduction showing incomplete conquest and the cycle (1:1-3:6), the stories of twelve judges (3:7-16:31), and two epilogues showing Israel's moral collapse (17-21).

## themes
- The Cycle of Sin | Israel sins, God allows oppression, Israel cries out, God raises a deliverer, and there is peace until they sin again (2:11-19).
- God's Patient Mercy | Again and again God hears His people's cry and rescues them, even when their repentance is shallow.
- Flawed Deliverers | God uses weak and flawed people like a left-handed man, a fearful farmer, an outcast, and a reckless strongman.
- The Spirit of the LORD | The Spirit empowers judges for deliverance (3:10; 6:34; 14:6).
- Moral Relativism | "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (21:25) leads to idolatry, violence, and chaos.
- The Need for a King | The book points to Israel's need for a righteous king, ultimately Christ.

## christ
- Deliverers | Each judge is a partial, flawed savior who points to the perfect Deliverer.
- 13:5 | Samson, a Nazirite from birth, begins to save Israel and wins his greatest victory in his death (compare Hebrews 2:14-15).
- 21:25 | The cry for a king is answered in David and ultimately in Jesus, the righteous King (Isaiah 9:6-7).
- 6:24 | Gideon's altar "The LORD Is Peace" points to Christ, our peace (Ephesians 2:14).

## reflect
- Where do you see the cycle of Judges (drifting, trouble, crying out, rescue) in your own life?
- Gideon felt like the least in his family, yet God called him a mighty man of valor. How does God see you differently than you see yourself?
- Samson's strength was undone by compromise. What small compromises could grow into bigger falls for you?
- "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Where are you following your own judgment rather than God's word?
- God used flawed people. How does that encourage you to serve despite your weaknesses?
## chapters
@ Incomplete Conquest
After Joshua's death, Judah and Simeon have success, and Caleb's family takes Hebron and Debir. But tribe after tribe fails to drive out the Canaanites, settling to live among them or put them to forced labor.
? What pattern do you notice as the chapter goes on?
? Why was partial obedience so dangerous for Israel?
? What areas of your life show "partial obedience"?
> Joshua 15:13-19 | Numbers 33:55 | Joshua 23:12-13
@ Israel's Unfaithfulness and the Cycle
The angel of the LORD rebukes Israel at Bochim for making covenants with the Canaanites. A new generation arises "who did not know the LORD." The cycle is introduced: Israel serves the Baals, God gives them to enemies, then raises up judges to save them, but they keep turning away.
? How can a whole generation grow up not knowing the LORD (2:10)?
? Describe the cycle in 2:11-19 in your own words.
? Why does God continue to rescue such an unfaithful people?
> Deuteronomy 6:6-12 | Psalm 78:5-8 | Psalm 106:43-45
@ Othniel, Ehud, and Shamgar
God leaves nations to test Israel. Othniel, empowered by the Spirit, delivers Israel from Mesopotamia. Ehud, a left-handed Benjamite, kills fat King Eglon of Moab with a hidden sword. Shamgar strikes down 600 Philistines with an oxgoad.
? Why did God leave nations in the land (3:1-4)?
? How does God use Ehud's left-handedness?
? What unusual "tools" has God given you to use for Him?

@ Deborah and Barak
Israel is oppressed by Jabin and his commander Sisera with 900 iron chariots. The prophetess Deborah, who judges Israel, summons Barak, who will only go if she goes. God routs Sisera, who flees and is killed by Jael with a tent peg.
? What do you notice about Deborah's leadership?
? Why did Barak lose the honor (4:9), and yet he is in Hebrews 11:32?
? How does God use unexpected people to bring victory?
> Hebrews 11:32-34 | 1 Corinthians 1:27 | Psalm 83:9-10
@ The Song of Deborah
Deborah and Barak sing praise to God for the victory, celebrating the volunteers who offered themselves, criticizing tribes that stayed home, and blessing Jael. The stars and the river Kishon fought against Sisera.
? Why does the song praise the people who "offered themselves willingly" (5:2, 9)?
? What does the criticism of Reuben, Dan, and Asher teach about staying uninvolved?
? How does the song close (5:31)?
> Exodus 15:1-21 | Romans 12:1 | Psalm 68:1-3
@ Gideon Called
Midian oppresses Israel for seven years. The angel of the LORD finds Gideon threshing wheat in a winepress and calls him "mighty man of valor." Gideon builds an altar named "The LORD Is Peace," tears down his father's Baal altar, and asks for signs with the fleece.
? Why do you think God called Gideon "mighty man of valor" while he was hiding?
? Why was tearing down the family altar the first step?
? What do you think of Gideon's fleece test? How should we seek guidance?

@ Gideon's Three Hundred
God reduces Gideon's army from 32,000 to 300 "lest Israel boast." Gideon overhears a Midianite's dream that confirms victory. With trumpets, jars, and torches, the 300 shout, "A sword for the LORD and for Gideon!" and Midian flees in confusion.
? Why did God reduce Gideon's army?
? How does God strengthen Gideon's faith through the overheard dream?
? Where might God be "reducing" your resources so that His power is seen?

Tola and Jair judge Israel. Israel serves many foreign gods and is oppressed by the Philistines and Ammonites. They cry out, and God says, "Go and cry out to the gods whom you have chosen." They put away their gods, and God "became impatient over the misery of Israel."
